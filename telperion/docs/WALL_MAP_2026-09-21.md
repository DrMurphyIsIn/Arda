## 0. One paragraph

On 2026-09-21 the campaign proved Weil's criterion in the kernel
(`zeta_comb_membership_iff_rh`, E6Bridge9): RH is equivalent to positivity of the
explicit-formula functional on Hermitian autocorrelations of smooth compactly supported
tests. The converse half went through a two-parameter Gaussian family, and that is the
seam. The assault below rewrites the Wall in those two real parameters, proves the part of
the parameter space that is free, builds the instrument that certifies the part the Turing
ladder reaches, and names exactly what is left. What is left is RH. The adversarial sweep
and its independent replication agree: the in-kernel Weil criterion changes the Wall's
coordinates, not its content.

## 1. The two-parameter Wall (seam A, E6Bridge10, blind audit PASS 6/6)

For a centre `c : R` and a width `lam > 0` let

    F(c, lam) := Re  Sum_rho  m(rho) (gamma_rho - c)^2 exp(-2 lam (gamma_rho - c)^2),
    gamma_rho := (rho - 1/2)/i   (real iff rho is on the critical line).

Kernel theorems (all `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`):

| theorem | statement in words | registry node |
|---|---|---|
| `rh_iff_gaussian_positivity` | RH  <->  for all c and all lam > 0, F(c, lam) >= 0 | `MM_rh_iff_gaussian_positivity` (proved) |
| `zeroSide_gaussTest_eq` | F(c, lam) = archSide - primeSide of the autocorrelation of the Gaussian-derivative test `gaussPhi c lam` (the explicit formula for a non-compactly-supported test, as the limit of E8 on truncations; dominated convergence on all three sides) | `MM_gaussian_explicit_formula` (proved) |
| `rh_iff_gaussian_prime_le_arch` | RH  <->  for all c, lam > 0: prime side <= archimedean side of that one test | `MM_rh_iff_gaussian_prime_le_arch` (proved) |

Reading: the infinite-dimensional quantifier over test functions is gone. The Wall is the
sign of one explicit function on the (c, lam) half-plane. Under RH every term is a
nonnegative real. Each off-line zero would carve a negative region (E6Bridge7,
`gaussian_dominance`), and the prime-side form has no zeros in it at all.

## 2. The free region (seam B, E6Bridge11 + E6Bridge13, blind audit in flight)

    lam0 := 1e-7.

`re_weilForm_gauss_nonneg`: for EVERY real c and every 0 < lam <= lam0, the archimedean
side of the Gaussian-derivative test exceeds its prime side. Unconditional. Mechanism:
archimedean dominance. The prime side is bounded by `64 A exp(-(log 2)^2/(16 lam))`, the
line integral of `|h|^2 Re psi(1/4 + i r/2)` is bounded below c-uniformly through Zeta23's
Stirling bounds on the digamma function, and the two pole terms decay in c. The threshold
is crude (numerics put the true c-uniform threshold near 0.1 and the pointwise one above 1
for c >= 500); the mechanism is not.

`gaussian_positivity_small_lam` (E6Bridge13, and now also inside E6Bridge11 itself): the
same region on the zero side, obtained by discharging seam B's one hypothesis with seam A's
Gaussian explicit formula.

The ENVELOPE form (E6Bridge11, `gaussian_positivity_envelope`): for every width lam > 0 there
is an explicit height `envelopeC lam` such that F(c, lam) >= 0 for every centre with
|c| >= envelopeC lam. Mechanism: archimedean dominance at large height, Re psi(1/4 + i r/2)
>= log(|r|/2) - 5 against a lam-uniform prime bound 16 A e^{16 lam}. The constant carries the
crude e^{16 lam} (the sharp c1(lam) ~ 2 pi e^{2 P(lam)} is the sweep's probe P3, not
attempted). So the free region is the UNION of the strip lam <= lam0 and the large-height
region |c| >= envelopeC(lam), for every lam.

Yoshida-type positivity. The Yoshida 1992 primary text was not accessed (UNRESOLVED in the
seam B memo); the Connes-Consani archimedean-place theorem (arXiv 2006.13771, verified from
the PDF by the skeptic) is the same foothold in support coordinates.

## 3. The ladder-certified region (seam C, E6Bridge12, blind audit PASS 7/7)

`WindowOnLine c D := every nontrivial zero with |Im rho - c| <= D has Re rho = 1/2`.
This is what the Turing ladder certifies for D <= |c| <= T - D (T = 640000 today), and it
enters the island as a HYPOTHESIS: the two islands run different toolchains, so the
instantiation is a registry-level composition, never a Lean import.

| theorem | statement in words |
|---|---|
| `gaussian_positivity_of_window` | WindowOnLine c D, one certified zero at distance in [delta, d] of c with d^2 + 1/4 < D^2, and lam >= lamThreshold(c, D, d, delta)  =>  F(c, lam) >= 0. Threshold explicit and log-free. Registered as `MM_gaussian_positivity_of_window` (proved). |
| `gaussian_positivity_of_window_dominance` | WindowOnLine c D, lam >= 1, and the FINITE certified window sum >= the explicit tail envelope `exp(2 (lam - 1)(1/4 - D^2)) * constB c`  =>  F(c, lam) >= 0. This is the form the numerics validate (the window sum beats the tail for lam >= 0.27 at D = 2); it is not registrable on the v4.32 statement island because its vocabulary rests on a finiteness theorem. |

Two caveats carried in the memo: the envelope is valid for lam >= 1 only, and `constB c` is
an infinite sum whose numeric upper bound must come from the unconditional zero count, not
from the finite certificate.

## 4. The wall map theorem (E6Bridge13)

    rh_iff_gaussian_positivity_above_lam0 :
        RiemannHypothesis  <->  for all c, for all lam > lam0, F(c, lam) >= 0.

The region lam <= lam0 is free. Everything above it is the Wall. Seam C certifies, from
finite data, the strip |c| <= T - D of that residual for lam above its threshold. The
residual after all of that:

    R := { (c, lam) : |c| > T - D, lam > lam0, |c| < envelopeC(lam) }   union   the sub-threshold band.

    (envelopeC grows doubly exponentially in lam, so the large-height free region is thin;
    R is unbounded in c for every fixed lam above lam0.)

Shrinking R to nothing is RH. No instrument in the corpus shrinks it to nothing, and the
sweep explains why: every certifying mechanism factors through certified zeros (finite
height) or through archimedean dominance (small width).
