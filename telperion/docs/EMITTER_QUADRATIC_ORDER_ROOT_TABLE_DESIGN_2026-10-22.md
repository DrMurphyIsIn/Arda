# Emitter design: `quadratic_order_root_table` (2026-10-22)

`QuadraticOrderRootTableEmitter`, kind `quadratic_order_root_table`, module
`src/telperion/emit_quadratic_order_root_table.py`, prelude `QuadraticOrderRoot` (emitted from the
same module). It is survey item N3 of the CN-1 emitter-shape survey (phase 2, rev 2), ranked third,
built after the hand-written class-number-one proof and ported from its kernel-checked Lean.

`conjecture1_proved = False`. This kind certifies how rational primes split in an explicit quadratic
order and, when a small prime splits, that the class number is not one. It says nothing about the
Riemann hypothesis or any other open problem.

## The problem

The class-number-one proof needs, for each of 190 imaginary quadratic fields, one small prime that
splits, and for each of the nine fields with class number one, a short table of primes that do not
split. Both facts are about the same object: the polynomial `X^2 - t X + c` of the ring of integers
`Z[theta]` reduced modulo `p`. Whether it has a root mod `p` decides everything (Kummer-Dedekind):
a root with `p` not dividing the discriminant means `p` splits into two primes of residue degree one;
no root means `p` is inert, one prime of residue degree two; `p` dividing the discriminant means `p`
ramifies, one prime of residue degree one and ramification index two.

The hand-written proof had these facts in three places: the 190 `IsWitness` instances
(generated by `gen_witnesses.py`), the eleven `decide` calls in the Minkowski converse, and the
generic splitting theorems of `Foundations/Splitting.lean` and `Ramified.lean`, which were tied to
the particular orders `Z[omega]` of `Q(sqrt -m)`. Nothing in Telperion's registry states a
`ZMod p` quantifier or has a soundness lemma to ideals: `finite_decide` can state the divisibility
over `N` but has no consumer, `padic` states `p^k | n` by `norm_num`, a different fact, and
`PrimalityEmitter` (Pratt/Lucas) is the heavier primality route, not needed here where `norm_num`
decides `Nat.Prime p` for the primes in question. So this is a new kind, and the content of the kind
is its consumer lemmas.

## The shape

The input is the order, given by `t` in `{0, 1}` and `c >= 1` (so `d = 4c - t^2 >= 3`), and a list of
primes. For each prime the generator computes, by exact integer arithmetic, exactly one of three
faces and emits it as a pure-`N` (or `ZMod p`) statement that the kernel decides from the literals:

| face | emitted statement | proof | consumer (under `D : QuadInt K t c`) | conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RAMIFIED | `p ∣ 4 * c - t ^ 2` | `decide` | `D.ramified_of_dvd p hp hd` | one prime over `p`, `inertiaDeg = 1`, `ramificationIdx = 2` |
| SPLIT | `IsRootMod t c p y` | `decide` | `D.split_of_isRootMod p hp hpd` | two primes over `p`, each `inertiaDeg = 1` |
| INERT | `∀ z : ZMod p, z ^ 2 - t * z + c ≠ 0` | `decide` | `D.inert_of_no_root p hp` | one prime over `p`, `inertiaDeg = 2` |

Here `IsRootMod t c p y` is `t * y ≤ y ^ 2 + c ∧ p ∣ y ^ 2 + c - t * y`, the CN-1 way of writing
`p | y^2 - t y + c` without truncated subtraction, and `hp : p.Prime` is `by norm_num`,
`hd : t ^ 2 < 4 * c` and `hpd : ¬ p ∣ 4 * c - t ^ 2` are `by decide`.

A split prime with `4p <= d` and `p != c` is also a class-number witness. For it the generator emits

    theorem <n>_w_<p> : IsWitness t c p y := ⟨by norm_num, by decide, by decide, by decide, by decide⟩
    theorem <n>_classNumber_ne_one (D : QuadInt K t c) : classNumber K ≠ 1 :=
      classNumber_ne_one_of_isWitness D (by decide) <n>_w_<p>

where `IsWitness t c p y` is `p.Prime ∧ t * y ≤ y ^ 2 + c ∧ p ∣ y ^ 2 + c - t * y ∧ 4 * p ≤ 4 * c - t ^ 2 ∧ p ≠ c`,
CN-1's `IsWitness m p y` with `(tOf m, cOf m, dOf m)` written out as `(t, c, 4c - t^2)`.

The capstone `theorem <n>` is the conjunction of the faces (the table), with a kernel
statement-match gate `example : <same type> := <n>`. The `_kd` corollaries and the class-number
theorem are separate, each conditional on `QuadInt K t c`.

### The `QuadInt` interface

`QuadInt K t c` is the structure of SplitPrime.lean, with `t, c : N` instead of `Z`: a generator
`θ : 𝓞 K`, `Algebra.adjoin ℤ {θ} = ⊤`, `minpoly ℤ θ = X ^ 2 - C t * X + C c`, and the norm form
`Algebra.norm ℤ (x + y θ) = x ^ 2 + t x y + c y ^ 2`. Every field is a hypothesis. The emitted
theorems are conditional on it, and the project that builds the ring of integers discharges it
(CN-1 does so in `Finite/Bridge.lean`, `quadIntOfFoundations`, from the W-A foundations). This is
the emitter boundary the survey identified: it is what makes the kind reusable for other monogenic
quadratic orders, and it is why the emitter does not need to know what `K` is.

Restricting `t, c` to `N` is a specialisation of the interface, not a weakening of any theorem: the
Rabinowitsch lemma needs `t in {0, 1}` anyway, and `c >= 1` is forced by `4p <= d`. It removes every
cast-unification issue between the `N` faces and the `Z` norm form.

Not ported: `absNorm_maximal_cases`, `le_two_thirds_sqrt_of_le_minkowski`, `exists_theta` and
`classNumber_eq_one_of_mem_nine` (Converse.lean). Those are survey item E5, `minkowski_pid_witness`,
whose table is the INERT faces emitted here together with a cutoff `4d < 9B^2`; the consumer that
turns them into `classNumber K = 1` is a different kind and is not built. `Splitting.lean`'s
character-side theorems (`kroneckerChar_eq_one_iff_exists_root` and the `ncard_primesOver_eq_two_iff`
pair) are not ported either: they relate the faces to `χ_{-d}(p)`, which the emitter does not state.

Two hypotheses were added, both for soundness with `N` subtraction:

* `ramified_of_dvd` takes `t ^ 2 < 4 * c`. Without it, `p ∣ 4 * c - t ^ 2` with the truncated
  difference `0` would hold for every `p` whenever `t^2 > 4c` (a real quadratic order such as
  `X^2 - 3X + 1`), and the lemma would claim that every prime ramifies in `Q(sqrt 5)`. The emitter
  discharges it by `decide`; for `t in {0, 1}`, `c >= 1` it is always true.
* `split_of_isRootMod` takes `¬ p ∣ 4 * c - t ^ 2`. This is unprovable when `t^2 >= 4c` (the
  truncated difference is `0`, divisible by everything), so the lemma is sound as stated; the proof
  derives `t^2 <= 4c` from it before casting.

## Python side

`quadratic_order_root_table_certificate(t, c, primes, inert_cap=200)` classifies each prime
exactly (`d % p`, then the least `y < p` with `(y*y + c - t*y) % p == 0` by exhaustion) and
refuses `t` outside `{0, 1}`, `c < 1`, any float or non-integer, an empty or repeated prime list, a
composite `p` (trial division; `p > 10^6` is refused rather than tested probabilistically), and an
INERT prime above `inert_cap`. `verify_certificate` re-checks every face independently, with the
same integer facts the kernel will decide, and `emit_body` refuses a certificate that fails it. No
floats anywhere; no sympy numerics on the certificate path.

The example (`examples/quadratic_order_root_table/`) has five orders and 58 theorems:

| name | order | `d`, `h` | primes | faces | witnesses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `d8` | `X^2 + 2` | 8, 1 | 2, 3, 5, 7, 199 | ram, split (y = 1), inert, inert, inert | none |
| `d20` | `X^2 + 5` | 20, 2 | 2, 3, 7 | ram, split (y = 1), split (y = 3) | 3 |
| `d56` | `X^2 + 14` | 56, 4 | 2, 3, 5, 7 | ram, split (y = 1), split (y = 1), ram | 3, 5 |
| `d31` | `X^2 - X + 8` | 31, 3 | 2, 5, 7 | split (y = 0), split (y = 2), split (y = 3) | 2, 5, 7 |
| `d163` | `X^2 - X + 41` | 163, 1 | 2, 3, 5, 7, 41, 163, 193 | inert x4, split (y = 0), ram, inert | none |

`d8` is the control case. `d163`'s four inert faces are exactly the no-root `decide` calls of
CN-1's Minkowski converse for `m = 163`; `41` splits (`y = 0`) but `4 * 41 = 164 > 163`, so the
generator correctly emits no witness for a field with class number one. The witnesses agree with
CN-1's `w_5 : IsWitness 5 3 1`, `w_14 : IsWitness 14 3 1` and `w_31 : IsWitness 31 2 1` (we emit
`y = 0` for `m = 31`: both `0` and `1` are roots mod `2`).

## Stance: CERTIFICATE_SENSITIVE

The kernel recomputes every face from the literals, so in one sense nothing is trusted from Python.
But the split and witness faces carry the residue `y` as witness data, and a wrong `y` is exactly a
corrupted certificate: `decide` on `p ∣ y ^ 2 + c - t * y` is then false and the theorem does not
compile. That is the definition of a corruptible witness, so the stance is CERTIFICATE_SENSITIVE,
with the registered adapter `negctrl_adapters/adapter_quadratic_order_root_table.py`.

### Negative control (kernel)

The order `X^2 + 5` (`t = 0`, `c = 5`, `d = 20`, `Q(sqrt -5)`, `h = 2`) at `p = 3`. The honest root is
`y = 1` (`1 + 5 = 6`) and it is a witness (`12 <= 20`, `3 != 5`). The forgery sets `y = 0`:
`0 + 5 = 5` is not divisible by `3`, so `decide` fails on `IsRootMod 0 5 3 0` and on the third
conjunct of `IsWitness 0 5 3 0`, and the kernel rejects. Layer 1 cannot mint this (`root_mod` computes
`y`, `verify_certificate` rejects the forged face, `emit_body` refuses it), so the adapter edits the
frozen dataclass by hand and renders through `_render_instance` directly. The true twin compiles
clean; the two emitted texts differ only in the lines carrying the literal `y`.

Run on 2026-10-22 against the built island (`generic_negative_control`, harness result
`kernel_rejects = True`, `true_compiles = True`, `okay = True`). The kernel's output on the forged
twin is two errors and nothing else:

    error: Tactic `decide` proved that the proposition
      IsRootMod 0 5 3 0
    is false
    error: Tactic `decide` proved that the proposition
      3 ∣ 0 ^ 2 + 5 - 0 * 0
    is false

(the split face and the third conjunct of the witness face); the true twin elaborates with exit 0
and `#print axioms` shows only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`.

### Structural control (REVIEW_E C2)

The predicate `IsWitness` lives in Lean, so no generator can weaken it; the control that matters is
on the prelude. Drop `p ≠ c` and the soundness lemma `classNumber_ne_one_of_isWitness` becomes FALSE:
for `d = 8` (`t = 0`, `c = 2`), `p = 2 = c`, `y = 0`, the remaining conjuncts all hold (`2` is prime,
`0 ≤ 0 + 2`, `2 ∣ 0 + 2`, `4 * 2 ≤ 8`) while `h(-8) = 1`. The ramified prime `p = d/4` IS represented by
the principal form, `Q(0, 1) = c`, so Rabinowitsch's argument does not apply to it. The prelude
records both halves as kernel-checked theorems:

    theorem not_isWitness_two_two : ¬ IsWitness 0 2 2 0 := fun h => h.2.2.2.2 rfl
    theorem isWitness_two_two_other_conjuncts :
        Nat.Prime 2 ∧ 0 * 0 ≤ 0 ^ 2 + 2 ∧ 2 ∣ 0 ^ 2 + 2 - 0 * 0 ∧ 4 * 2 ≤ 4 * 2 - 0 ^ 2

`p ≠ c` is the only conjunct that refuses the instance. Where it bites: for `t = 0`, `p = c` divides
`d = 4c`, so `p` is the ramified double root and the bound `4p ≤ d` holds with equality. For `t = 1`,
`p = c` can split (`X^2 - X + 2` at `p = 2`: `y^2 - y + 2` is even for every `y`), but then
`4p = 4c > d = 4c - 1`, so the bound already refuses. Hence the generator never produces a split
prime with `4p ≤ d` and `p = c`, and the control case `d8` emits `p = 2` as RAMIFIED with no
witness. Rev 1's control for this item (a generator loosening `4p ≤ d` to `4p ≤ d + 1`) was withdrawn
in the survey and is not used here.

### Instance-level controls (tests, Lean-gated)

`IsWitness 0 2 2 0` cannot be proved by the emitted tactic block (`2 ≠ 2` fails), while
`IsRootMod 0 2 2 0` CAN (the root exists; it is the double root). The forged root at `p = 3` is
kernel-rejected and the honest twin compiles (the adapter). Both run in
`tests/test_emit_quadratic_order_root_table.py` when the island is built.

## Kernel cost (open check 4 of the survey)

The RAMIFIED and SPLIT faces are one `N` divisibility each and are negligible. The INERT face is
`decide` over `ZMod p`, which enumerates the `p` residues. Measured on this machine (Apple silicon,
Lean v4.32.0, `lake env lean` with `set_option profiler true` on a file holding one face after
`import QuadraticOrderRoot`; the import itself is about 5 s and is not counted):

| `p` | order | `decide` (elaboration) | kernel type checking | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | `X^2 + 2` | below the 10 ms profiler threshold | below threshold | the CN-1 Minkowski table |
| 101 | `X^2 + 2` | 38 ms | 25 ms | |
| 191 | `X^2 + 2` | 73 ms | 54 ms | |
| 193 | `X^2 - X + 41` | 70 ms | 50 ms | `d163` |
| 397 | `X^2 + 2` | 156 ms | 109 ms | |
| 1013 | `X^2 + 2` | 416 ms | 307 ms | |

Linear in `p`, as expected, and cheap: a thousand-residue field is under half a second. The one
practical limit is not the kernel but Lean's default elaboration recursion depth: at the default
`maxRecDepth 512` the `decide` fails with "maximum recursion depth has been reached" for `p = 193`
and `p = 199` (it passes at `p = 101`). So for `p > 100` the INERT face is emitted under
`set_option maxRecDepth 8192 in` (an elaboration depth, not a kernel one; CN-1 used the same idiom
for its 50-way `rcases`), and with it every prime through 1013 passes. The default cap is
`MAX_INERT_PRIME = 1024`, and `inert_cap` raises it explicitly; above that the measurement stops,
not the method. The Legendre-symbol route the survey names as a fallback (a `norm_num` extension
for `jacobiSym`) was not needed at these sizes; nothing here precludes adding it as a second proof
of the same statement for much larger `p`.

## What is NOT guaranteed

* Every `_kd` and class-number theorem is conditional on `QuadInt K t c`. The emitter does not prove
  that any field has that ring of integers; that is the consumer's job (CN-1's Bridge does it for
  `Q(sqrt -m)`).
* `classNumber K ≠ 1` is the only class-number statement. Nothing about `h ≤ 2`, `h = h0`, or
  `classNumber K = 1` (that is E5, not built). `not_form_eq_prime` is about the principal form only
  and does not generalise to non-principal forms without new Lean.
* The faces are about the order given by `(t, c)`, which must be the maximal order for the `_kd`
  statements to be about `𝓞 K`; `QuadInt.adjoin_eq_top` carries that.
* `t` is restricted to `{0, 1}`. A monogenic quadratic order always has such a reduced form (shift
  `θ`), but the emitter does not perform the shift.
* Primality of `p` is exact trial division up to `10^6`; larger primes are refused, not tested.
