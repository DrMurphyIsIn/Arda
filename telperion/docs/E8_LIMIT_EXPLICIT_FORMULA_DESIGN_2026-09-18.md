*Authored 2026-09-17 (memo dated for the 2026-09-18 queue slot it fills). Statement
authored, NOT proved. The node is `draft`, its statement file ends in the by-design
`sorry`, and nothing here is evidence for or against RH.*

This memo does three things the roadmap said had to happen before E8 could be
registered: it fixes the test class, it fixes the normalisation of every term, and
it shows the statement survives the two refutations in
`RH_ROUTES_ROADMAP_2026-09-16.md` (section 1 and section 8) that killed the naive

## 0. The statement (as registered)

Node `RH_limit_explicit_formula`, kind `milestone`, deps `RH_rvm_unconditional`,
`RH_corridor_bound`. Vocabulary in the AUTHORED block `WeilExplicit` of
`missions/rh/lean/Statements/RHDefs.lean` (generated by `build_rhdefs.py`).

```lean
namespace WeilExplicit

def IsWeilTest (g : ℝ → ℂ) : Prop :=
  ContDiff ℝ ((⊤ : ℕ∞) : WithTop ℕ∞) g ∧ HasCompactSupport g

noncomputable def weilKernel (g : ℝ → ℂ) (s : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  ∫ u : ℝ, g u * Complex.exp ((s - 1 / 2) * (u : ℂ))

noncomputable def zeroMult (ρ : ℂ) : ℕ :=
  ((MeromorphicOn.divisor riemannZeta {s : ℂ | 0 < s.re ∧ s.re < 1} : ℂ → ℤ) ρ).toNat

noncomputable def archIntegrand (g : ℝ → ℂ) (r : ℝ) : ℂ :=
  weilKernel g (1 / 2 + (r : ℂ) * I) * ((Complex.digamma (1 / 4 + ((r : ℂ) / 2) * I)).re : ℂ)

noncomputable def archSide (g : ℝ → ℂ) : ℂ :=
  weilKernel g 0 + weilKernel g 1 - g 0 * (Real.log Real.pi : ℂ)
    + (1 / (2 * (Real.pi : ℂ))) * ∫ r : ℝ, archIntegrand g r

noncomputable def primeSide (g : ℝ → ℂ) : ℂ :=
  ∑' n : ℕ, ((ArithmeticFunction.vonMangoldt n / Real.sqrt n : ℝ) : ℂ)
    * (g (Real.log n) + g (-Real.log n))

end WeilExplicit

theorem limit_explicit_formula (g : ℝ → ℂ) (hg : WeilExplicit.IsWeilTest g) :
    Integrable (WeilExplicit.archIntegrand g) ∧
    HasSum (fun ρ : ℂ => (WeilExplicit.zeroMult ρ : ℂ) * WeilExplicit.weilKernel g ρ)
      (WeilExplicit.archSide g - WeilExplicit.primeSide g) := by sorry
```

In classical notation, with `ρ = 1/2 + iγ_ρ` and `h(r) = ∫ g(u) e^{iru} du` (so
`H_g(ρ) = weilKernel g ρ = h(γ_ρ)`, `H_g(0) = h(i/2)`, `H_g(1) = h(-i/2)`):

```
Σ_ρ m(ρ) h(γ_ρ) = h(i/2) + h(-i/2) - g(0) log π + (1/2π) ∫ h(r) Re ψ(1/4 + ir/2) dr
                  - Σ_n Λ(n)/√n (g(log n) + g(-log n))
```

This is exactly the shape proposed in `RH_CRITICAL_PATH_NODES_2026-09-17.md`
("Deliberately NOT registered: E8"), with two deliberate changes: `g` is
complex-valued and there is **no evenness hypothesis** (section 3.4 explains why
both are free and why B7 needs them).

## 1. What E8 is the limit of

The in-tree finite explicit formula is `DiffractionCore.rect_explicit_formula`
(`RvMDiffractionCore.lean`, brick 6, on `rh/million-turing`): for a box
`[σ₀, σ₁] × [T₀, T₁]` with `σ₁ > 1`, zero-free edges, and a holomorphic weight `g`
on a neighbourhood of the box,

```
2πi Σ_{ρ in box} d(ρ) g(ρ) = (bottom edge) - (top edge)
                              - i Σ'_n ∫_{T₀}^{T₁} g(σ₁+iy) Λ(n) n^{-σ₁-iy} dy
                              - i (left edge),
```

each edge an integral of `g · ζ'/ζ`. The Bragg bridge `rect_explicit_formula_bragg`
(`RvMBraggBridge.lean`) is its `g ≡ 1` face, where the prime edge collapses to the
closed-form comb `Σ' braggTerm`. That face has **no** `T → ∞` limit: its zero side
is the box count `N(T)`, which diverges like `T log T`. A limit exists only against
a weight that decays along the vertical edges and kills the horizontal ones.

E8 takes the weight `g_E8(s) := H_g(s) = weilKernel g s` for a test function `g`
in the class of section 2, on the box `[-δ, 1+δ] × [-T', T']` with `T'` running
through the corridor heights of `RH_corridor_bound` (the bottom edge at `-T'` is
zero-free with the same bound by conjugate symmetry `ζ(s̄) = conj ζ(s)`), and lets
`T' → ∞`. The dictionary between the finite terms and the E8 terms:

| finite term (`rect_explicit_formula`) | `T' → ∞` | E8 term |
|---|---|---|
| zero side `Σ_{box} d(ρ) H_g(ρ)` | absolutely convergent (section 5) | `HasSum (zeroMult · weilKernel g)` |
| bottom, top edges | `→ 0`: `|H_g(σ ± iT')| ≤ C_g/T'^2`, `|ζ'/ζ| ≤ C log² T'` on the corridor | absent |
| prime (right) edge at `σ₁ = 1+δ` | Fourier inversion, term by term | `Σ_n Λ(n)/√n g(log n)` |
| left edge at `σ₀ = -δ`, after `ζ'/ζ(s) = -ζ'/ζ(1-s) + log π - ½ψ(s/2) - ½ψ((1-s)/2)` | Dirichlet series of `ζ'/ζ(1-s)` | `Σ_n Λ(n)/√n g(-log n)` (the second prime copy) |
| same, the `log π` piece | shift to `Re s = 1/2` | `-g(0) log π` |
| same, the two `ψ` pieces | shift to `Re s = 1/2`; the `ψ(s/2)` shift crosses its pole at `s = 0` | `(1/2π)∫ h Re ψ(1/4+ir/2)` and `h(i/2)` |
| pole of `ζ` at `s = 1` inside the box | residue | `h(-i/2)` |

So "the two prime terms `g(log n) + g(-log n)`" are literally the two vertical edges,
and the "diffraction form" survives the limit: the prime side is the Bragg comb
`Σ_n Λ(n)/√n (δ_{log n} + δ_{-log n})` paired with `g`, the zero side is the zero comb
`Σ_ρ m(ρ) δ_{γ_ρ}` paired with `h`, the archimedean term is the absolutely continuous
density `(1/2π) Re ψ(1/4 + ir/2) dr` paired with `h`, plus the two point masses at
`r = ±i/2` and the `-log π δ₀` on the `g` side. That triple (zero comb, prime comb,
archimedean density) is what Route A's "regularized triple" (A0) names; E8 is the
identity between its three pairings, on this test class.

## 2. The test class: smooth compactly supported `g : ℝ → ℂ`, direct side

### 2.1 The three candidates and the choice

| candidate | where the primitive object lives | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| (1) even Schwartz `h` on ℝ with compactly supported Fourier transform | zero side (`h` primitive) | same class as (2) as a set of pairs `(g, h)`; wrong coordinate for Lean (section 2.3) |
| (2) smooth compactly supported `g` on ℝ, `h := ∫ g e^{iru}` | prime side (`g` primitive) | **chosen** |
| (3) Guinand-Weil: `h` holomorphic in `|Im r| ≤ 1/2 + δ`, `h(r) = O((1+|r|)^{-2-δ})` | zero side, with a strip-holomorphy predicate | larger class, deferred (section 2.4) |

(1) and (2) are the same class by Paley-Wiener (a Schwartz function whose Fourier
transform has compact support is exactly the restriction to ℝ of the entire
transform of a `C_c^∞` function). They differ in which side is *defined* and which
is *derived*, and that difference is the whole Lean design.

### 2.2 Defense against the PNT-growth refutation (roadmap section 1 / section 8)

The refuted statement was "the dual comb on `{±m log p}` with amplitudes
`(log p) p^{-m/2}` is tempered", false because `Σ_{n ≤ e^R} Λ(n) n^{-1/2} ~ 2 e^{R/2}`.
E8 never asserts temperedness. Its prime side pairs that comb with a **compactly
supported** `g`, so `primeSide g` is a finite sum (for `supp g ⊂ [-R, R]` only
`n ≤ e^R` contribute; `Λ(0) = Λ(1) = 0`). The exponential growth is exactly why
the class must be compactly supported on the `g` side and not merely Schwartz:
for a Schwartz `g` with, say, `g(u) = exp(-(1+u²)^{1/4})`,
`Σ_n Λ(n) n^{-1/2} g(log n)` diverges (compare `∫ e^{x/2 - √x} dx`). A "Schwartz `g`"
version of E8 would be false-or-undefined on the prime side; this class is the
honest one. (The Guinand class (3) is the largest classical class precisely because
strip-holomorphy of `h` to width `1/2 + δ` corresponds to `g` decaying like
`e^{-(1/2+δ)|u|}`, which just beats the `e^{u/2}` comb growth.)

### 2.3 Defense against the Paley-Wiener refutation (roadmap D3 / section 8)

The refuted statement (D3 as first drafted) put a band-limited `h` on the zero side
and asked for positivity below a certified height. The obstruction: to *see* an
off-line zero `ρ = β + it` the test transform must be evaluated at the complex
ordinate `γ = t - i(β - 1/2)`, so `h` must be analytic in `|Im r| ≤ 1/2`; a function
given only on the real `r`-line (band-limited or not) has no canonical value there.

E8 sidesteps this by construction: `h` is never primitive. `weilKernel g s` is *one
Bochner integral* of a compactly supported continuous integrand, defined for every
`s ∈ ℂ` by the same formula, and it is entire (Paley-Wiener in the harmless
direction). The value at an off-line zero is literal, not a continuation. The lead's
framing, "band-limited dies as a positivity class but is fine as an identity class",
is made precise by the coordinate choice: the class is fine for the identity *and*
for Weil's positivity criterion (Bombieri 2000 states Weil's criterion on exactly
`C_c^∞(ℝ)`, and the roadmap's B9 "`2L ≤ log 2`" support bound is a statement about
this class), because in `g`-coordinates the off-line values exist. What dies is the
`h`-primitive band-limited *positivity* statement, which is D3's problem, not E8's.

### 2.4 Why not the Guinand class (3) now

Formalizing (3) needs a predicate "`h` extends holomorphically to the strip", an
existential over extensions, plus a decay hypothesis on the extension. Every
consumer would have to discharge that existential, and every junk-value audit would
have to cover it. Class (2) is a subclass of (3) with no such predicate, it is the
class Weil 1952 and Iwaniec-Kowalski Thm 5.12 state the formula on, and the
extension (2) → (3) is a separate limiting argument (dominated convergence against
the absolutely convergent zero sum) that can be its own node once E8 is proved.
Registering (3) first would put the extension's bookkeeping inside the statement.

### 2.5 Smoothness: `C^∞`, and the `ω` trap

Weil's class is `C_c^∞`. Mathlib's `ContDiff ℝ n` takes `n : WithTop ℕ∞`; the top
element `⊤ = ω` means *analytic*, and an analytic function with compact support on
ℝ is identically zero. Writing `ContDiff ℝ ⊤ g` would collapse the class to `{0}`
and make the theorem vacuously true. The class uses `((⊤ : ℕ∞) : WithTop ℕ∞)`
(smooth), and the probe file checks `((⊤ : ℕ∞) : WithTop ℕ∞) ≠ ⊤` and
`contDiff_infty`. The proof route only needs `C²` (two integrations by parts,
section 5); a later weakening to `C_c²` would strengthen the theorem and cannot hurt
a consumer, but the registered statement matches the literature class.

## 3. Exact normalisation (Weil 1952 / Guinand 1948 / Iwaniec-Kowalski 5.12)

### 3.1 Derivation

Let `Λ(s) = π^{-s/2} Γ(s/2) ζ(s)` (Mathlib `completedRiemannZeta`), `Λ(s) = Λ(1-s)`,
simple poles at `0, 1`, zeros exactly the nontrivial zeros of `ζ` with the same
multiplicities. Put `H(s) = H_g(s) = ∫ g(u) e^{(s-1/2)u} du`, so `H(1/2 + ir) = h(r)`.

Integrate `H · Λ'/Λ` around the strip `-δ ≤ Re s ≤ 1+δ`:

```
I := (1/2πi) [ ∫_{(1+δ)} - ∫_{(-δ)} ] H(s) Λ'/Λ(s) ds = Σ_ρ m(ρ) H(ρ) - H(0) - H(1)
```

(zeros: residue `+m(ρ)`; the two poles of `Λ`: residue `-1` each). By the functional
equation, `Λ'/Λ(s) = -Λ'/Λ(1-s)`, and substituting `s = 1 - w` on the left line
(orientation reverses once, the sign of `Λ'/Λ` once more):

```
(1/2πi) ∫_{(-δ)} H(s) Λ'/Λ(s) ds = -(1/2πi) ∫_{(1+δ)} H(1-w) Λ'/Λ(w) dw .
```

`H(1-w) = ∫ g(u) e^{-(w-1/2)u} du = H_{g̃}(w)` with `g̃(u) = g(-u)`. Hence

```
Σ_ρ m(ρ) H_g(ρ) = H_g(0) + H_g(1) + (1/2πi) ∫_{(1+δ)} H_{g+g̃}(w) Λ'/Λ(w) dw .
```

On `Re w = 1+δ`, `Λ'/Λ(w) = -½ log π + ½ ψ(w/2) - Σ_n Λ(n) n^{-w}`. Term by term:

* prime: `(1/2πi) ∫_{(c)} H_f(w) n^{-w} dw = f(log n)/√n` (Fourier inversion at
  `u = log n`, the `e^{-u/2}` from `e^{(w-1/2)u}`), so the prime term is
  `-Σ_n Λ(n)/√n (g(log n) + g(-log n))`;
* `log π`: `-½ log π · (1/2πi)∫_{(1/2)} H_{g+g̃} = -½ log π · (g + g̃)(0) = -g(0) log π`
  (shift to `Re w = 1/2`, no poles; `(1/2π)∫ h = g(0)`);
* archimedean: `½ (1/2πi) ∫_{(1/2)} H_{g+g̃}(w) ψ(w/2) dw = (1/2π) ∫ ½(h(r) + h(-r)) ψ(1/4 + ir/2) dr`
  (no poles of `ψ(w/2)` in `1/2 ≤ Re w ≤ 1+δ`), and substituting `r → -r` in the
  `h(-r)` half with `ψ(z̄) = conj ψ(z)` gives `(1/2π) ∫ h(r) Re ψ(1/4 + ir/2) dr`.

So, with no parity assumption on `g`,

```
Σ_ρ m(ρ) H_g(ρ) = H_g(0) + H_g(1) - g(0) log π + (1/2π) ∫ H_g(1/2+ir) Re ψ(1/4+ir/2) dr
                  - Σ_n Λ(n)/√n (g(log n) + g(-log n)) .
```

Every symbol maps to one `WeilExplicit` definition: `H_g = weilKernel g`,
`m = zeroMult`, the integrand is `archIntegrand g`, the first four terms are
`archSide g`, the last is `primeSide g`, and `ψ = Complex.digamma = logDeriv Gamma`
(Mathlib, `Gamma/Digamma.lean` at the pin).

### 3.2 Equivalent literature forms

* Weil 1952 / Bombieri 2000 use the multiplicative variable `x = e^u` and
  `f̂(s) = ∫_0^∞ f(x) x^{s-1} dx`; with `f(x) = g(log x)/√x` this is `H_g(s)`.
* Iwaniec-Kowalski Thm 5.12 for `ζ` (conductor 1, `γ(s) = π^{-s/2}Γ(s/2)`) with
  `h(r) = ∫ g(u) e^{iru} du` is the displayed form verbatim.
* Guinand 1948 is the same identity with the archimedean term written as an
  integral against `Re ψ`; the "Re" is forced by the symmetrisation
  `(h(r) + h(-r))/2`, it is not a real-valuedness assumption on `g`.

### 3.3 The `Re ψ` term versus the corpus

Roadmap D2 (skeptic): the Bragg bridge "has NO archimedean Γ term" because it is
stated for the uncompleted `ζ` with edge remainders. In E8 the archimedean term is
where those remainders go: it is the `ψ(s/2)` and `ψ((1-s)/2)` pieces of the left
edge after the functional equation. `archIntegrand` is therefore the registry's
first named archimedean object; D2's Weil-Gram matrix can cite it.

### 3.4 Evenness and complex values are dropped, on purpose

The derivation used only the functional equation, so evenness is not needed; the
`g(log n) + g(-log n)` and `h(i/2) + h(-i/2)` pairs are the general form (for even
`g` they collapse to `2g(log n)` and `2h(i/2)`). Weil's positivity functional is
`W(f ⋆ f̃)` with `f̃(x) = conj f(-x)`; `f ⋆ f̃` is Hermitian-even, so its imaginary
part is *odd* and its values are complex. An even real-valued class would not serve
B7 without a separate linearity-and-parity lemma; the registered class does.
Section 4 checks the non-even case numerically.

## 5. Absolute convergence of the zero side from the `O(log T)` count alone

Let `g` be in the class, `supp g ⊂ [-R, R]`. Two integrations by parts (boundary
terms vanish by compact support) give, for `s ≠ 1/2`,

```
H_g(s) = (s - 1/2)^{-2} ∫ g''(u) e^{(s-1/2)u} du,   so   |H_g(ρ)| ≤ C_g / |ρ - 1/2|²,
C_g := e^{R/2} ∫ |g''| ,
```

uniformly for `0 < Re ρ < 1` (there `|e^{(ρ-1/2)u}| ≤ e^{|u|/2}`). For `ρ = β + it`,
`|ρ - 1/2|² ≥ t²`. Split the zeros at `|t| = 1`:

* `|t| ≤ 1`: finitely many (`N(1) < ∞`, from `RH_rvm_unconditional`; in fact zero,
  but that is not needed), each with `|H_g(ρ)| ≤ e^{R/2} ∫|g|`.
* `|t| > 1`: by conjugate symmetry (`ζ(s̄) = conj ζ(s)`, so `m(ρ̄) = m(ρ)`) it
  suffices to bound `Σ_{t > 1} m(ρ)/t² = ∫_1^∞ t^{-2} dN(t) = 2 ∫_1^∞ N(t) t^{-3} dt`
  (Stieltjes by parts, `N(t) t^{-2} → 0`). `RH_rvm_unconditional` gives
  `N(t) ≤ (t/2π) log(t/2π) + C log t + 1 ≤ A t log t` for `t ≥ 2`, and
  `∫ t^{-2} log t dt < ∞`.

Hence `Σ_ρ m(ρ) |H_g(ρ)| < ∞`. In ℂ (finite-dimensional) absolute summability is
summability, so `HasSum` holds with the sum equal to the limit along *any*
exhaustion, in particular along the symmetric corridor boxes `[-T', T']` that the
finite formula produces. This is why the "conditional-convergence bookkeeping
(symmetric zero pairing)" the roadmap flags for E8 does **not** arise on this
class: pairing is needed for classes where `h` decays only like `1/|r|` (for
example the Bombieri-Lagarias functions of section 6), not here. The only inputs
used are the upper half of the RvM remainder and conjugate symmetry.

Archimedean integrability (the first conjunct): on the line, `|h(r)| ≤ C_g/r²` by
the same bound, and `|Re ψ(1/4 + ir/2)| ≤ log(2 + |r|) + C` (Stirling), so
`archIntegrand g` is integrable. Both facts are theorems for the class; the
statement asserts them so a reader does not have to trust the Bochner-integral
and `tsum` conventions (section 7).

## 6. Consumers this shape serves

* **B6** (the `T → ∞` Weil form as a kernel identity): this node *is* B6; the
  roadmap's "test-class choice determines the statement; queued until B5" is
  resolved by section 2.
* **B7-ii** (Bombieri-Lagarias analytic layer): E8 gives the identity on `C_c^∞`.
  BL's test functions for `λ_n` are `h_n(r) = 1 - (1 - 1/ρ)^n`, `ρ = 1/2 + ir`,
  which decay only like `n/|r|` and have a pole at `r = i/2`; they are in neither
  this class nor the Guinand class. The B6 → B7 edge therefore needs its own
  limiting node (dominated convergence from `C_c^∞` approximants with symmetric
  pairing), exactly the "admissibility gap" the roadmap flags. E8 does not close it
  and does not claim to.
* **D6** (corridor bound + full Guinand-Weil in Lean): `D6 = E7 + E8`. The `HasSum`
  form is the trace reading at infinite height: the trace of `H_g` over the
  spectrum on the left, the diffraction data on the right, the finite-height
  `rect_trace_reading` being the box-truncated instance.
* **D2** (primes-side Weil-Gram matrix): the archimedean term the skeptic found
  missing from the bridge is now `archIntegrand`; the Weil form `W(f)` for
  `f = g ⋆ g̃` is `archSide f - primeSide f` by E8, computable from the prime side
  and one digamma integral.
* **D3 (reformulated)**: the strip-analytic admissible class D3 needs is class (2)
  in `g`-coordinates; E8 supplies the identity on it, positivity remains D3's.
* **Route A** (A0/A3/A5): section 1's table is the "regularized triple" made
  concrete. E8 is the honest form of the membership *identity*: it says nothing
  about temperedness of any comb, only that three pairings agree on `C_c^∞`. The
  refuted temperedness statement is a different sentence and E8 does not imply it.
* **Route C**: no consumer.

## 8. Proof route (not attempted here; hard-known-shape per the roadmap)

1. `weilKernel g` is entire, with `|H_g(s)| ≤ C_g / |s - 1/2|²` on `|Re s - 1/2| ≤ 1`

2. Corridor boxes: from `RH_corridor_bound`, heights `T'_k ∈ [k, k+1]` with zero-free
   horizontal segments on `[-1, 2]` and `|ζ'/ζ| ≤ C log² T'_k`; conjugate symmetry
   for `-T'_k`; the horizontal edge integrals are `O(log² T'_k / T'_k²) → 0`.
3. Apply `rect_explicit_formula` with weight `H_g` on `[-δ, 1+δ] × [-T'_k, T'_k]`
   (the `hbox_ball`/`hins` hypotheses need a ball containing the box and avoiding
   `s = 1`; the box has `1` on its interior, so this step has to be re-cut with the
   pole handled explicitly, or `zetaPoleCompanion` used, as `RH_companion_bragg_reduction`
   does). This is the one place the finite brick does not fit verbatim.
4. Prime edge: term-by-term Fourier inversion `∫ H_g(σ₁ + iy) n^{-σ₁-iy} dy = 2π g(log n)/√n`
   and dominated convergence in `n` (finite sum, so trivial).
5. Left edge: functional equation for `ζ'/ζ`, the second prime copy, the `log π`

6. Zero side: absolute convergence (section 5) from `RH_rvm_unconditional`, then
   `HasSum` from the box limits.
