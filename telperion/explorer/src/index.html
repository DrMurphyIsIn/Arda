The quasicrystal

a

What a Fourier quasicrystal is

Take a discrete set of points on the real line and put a unit mass at each one. Call the result a comb. A comb is a Fourier quasicrystal when its Fourier transform is again a comb: a countable sum of point masses, the Bragg peaks of a diffraction picture, with no continuous part. A periodic lattice is the trivial example (Poisson summation). The interesting examples are not periodic. Kurasov and Sarnak (2020) showed that every Fourier quasicrystal with integer masses on the line is the real zero set of a Lee-Yang exponential polynomial, and Alon, Cohen and Vinzant (2024) showed the converse. The quasicrystal island carries the finite shadow of that dictionary in the kernel: a two-frequency exponential sum is real-rooted exactly when its two coefficients have equal modulus (node MM_twofreq_realrooted_iff), and a one-frequency Lee-Yang polynomial has a crystalline zero set (KSConstruction).

b

The dual comb picture

The Guinand-Weil explicit formula pairs two combs. On one side sit the ordinates of the nontrivial zeros of zeta, symmetrised and weighted by multiplicity. On the other side sit the prime powers at frequencies k log p with weights (log p) p^(-k/2), together with a smooth archimedean density and two pole terms. For an even test function g with transform h,

sum over zeros of h(gamma)  =  archimedean(h)  -  2 sum over p, k of (log p) p^(-k/2) g(k log p)  +  poles

The prime comb exists unconditionally and is, in the kernel, a finite-height identity with the zeros: node MM_bragg_bridge states the explicit formula in diffraction form for a rectangle, with no limit taken, and node RH_limit_explicit_formula states the full limit form (Weil 1952 / Guinand 1948) for smooth compactly supported tests. What is not known is whether the zero comb is real. Unconditionally the ordinates are complex numbers; the Riemann Hypothesis says they are all real.

c

Dyson's 2009 suggestion and the reverse-Dyson framing

In Birds and frogs (Notices of the AMS, 2009) Freeman Dyson suggested classifying one-dimensional quasicrystals, on the grounds that if the zeros of zeta lie on the critical line then their comb is a quasicrystal with the prime powers as its spectrum, so a classification would have to contain it. The MIRRORMERE campaign runs this in reverse. It does not assume the zeros are on the line. It asks which weakening of the Fourier-quasicrystal axioms is the smallest one that still admits the zeta comb and still excludes the known counterfeits, and it tries to locate exactly where the hypothesis of reality enters. Kurasov and Sarnak already proved that the classical class is the wrong target: the zeta comb is not uniformly discrete (its counting function grows like T log T), so it is provably not a classical Fourier quasicrystal. Node MM_primelog_spectrum_dense and node MM_zeta_ordinates_not_uniformly_discrete put that obstruction in the kernel.

The prime comb and its diffraction

The spectrum side of the explicit formula is the comb with mass Lambda(n) / sqrt(n) at u = log n, supported exactly on the prime powers. Summing its cosines gives a trigonometric polynomial in the height t; as more prime powers are included, it develops sharp dips at the zero ordinates. This is the picture Dyson had in mind, drawn from the bundled ordinates. The ordinates are a float model (mpmath zetazero), not the certified Arb ladder, and the sum is truncated at n <= N.

The negative control: Davenport-Heilbronn

The Davenport-Heilbronn function satisfies a functional equation of zeta's type but has no Euler product, and it has zeros off the critical line. It is the object every live axiom variant must reject. The certified inventory (zoo_data/dh_zeros.json, argument-principle winding numbers with Arb enclosures of the function and its derivative on every boundary segment, in interval arithmetic, not kernel) records every zero of the function up to height 300 together with its real part: 204 on the line and 5 off it. It proves its own completeness: the winding number of the whole rectangle [-1, 2] x [1/20, 300] is 214 = 204 + 2 x 5, and that leaves no room for another zero, because the listed boxes are disjoint, the on-line boxes are symmetric about the line, each off-line zero's mirror image across the line lies in no listed box, real part 2 and beyond is zero-free, and a small box about the real axis excludes heights below 1/20. The plot shows them beside zeta's bundled ordinates.

The wall

On 2026-09-21 the campaign put Weil's criterion in the kernel and then rewrote it in two real parameters, a centre c and a width lam. For the Gaussian-derivative test the zero side of the explicit formula is

F(c, lam) = Re sum over zeros of m(rho) (gamma - c)^2 exp(-2 lam (gamma - c)^2),   gamma = (rho - 1/2)/i

and node MM_rh_iff_gaussian_positivity says RH holds exactly when F(c, lam) >= 0 for every c and every lam > 0. Under RH every term is a nonnegative real. The wall is the sign of one explicit function on a half-plane.

The Theta face and heat monotonicity

The plain Gaussian face Theta(c, lam) = Re sum m(rho) exp(-2 lam (gamma - c)^2) is also equivalent to RH (node MM_rh_iff_theta_positivity) and, unlike F, is heat-monotone in the width: Theta(., lam') is a Gaussian convolution of Theta(., lam) for lam' < lam (node MM_theta_heat_monotone), so positivity on the whole line at one width implies it at every smaller width. Move the slider: the smaller-width curve is always a smoothed, rescaled copy of the larger-width one. The free widths form an interval, and RH says that interval is all of (0, infinity).

Li face

Li's criterion: RH holds exactly when every coefficient lambda_N of the Taylor expansion of log xi(1/(1 - z)) at z = 0 is nonnegative. The li_positivity island carries the upstream equivalence (Bulka's LiCriterion, pinned) and prices RH rung by rung. Each rung is a paired zero sum, and the Bombieri-Lagarias explicit formula (node RH_bl_explicit_formula, hypothesis-free) says what each rung is.

The de Bruijn-Newman constant

Polya's heat-flow family deforms the Riemann xi function by a time parameter t:

H_t(z) = integral from 0 to infinity of exp(t u^2) Phi(u) cos(z u) du,      H_0(z) = xi(1/2 + i z / 2) / 8

with Phi the Polymath15 series (the island's DBN.H, telperion/examples/dbn/lean/DBNDefs.lean). De Bruijn (1950) showed that once every zero of H_t is real, every zero of every later H_t' is real too. Newman (1976) showed that a constant Lambda exists with H_t real-rooted exactly when t ≥ Lambda. RH says that the zeros of H_0 are real, which is Lambda ≤ 0.

What this tab does not claim. Lambda ≤ 9/32 = 0.28125 is weaker than the published bounds Lambda ≤ 0.22 (Polymath15, 2019) and Lambda ≤ 0.2 (Platt-Trudgian, 2021). It is not RH: RH is Lambda ≤ 0, and with Newman's conjecture Lambda ≥ 0 (Rodgers-Tao's theorem, kernel-checked here along Dobner's proof as RH_dbn_newman) it is Lambda = 0. The published bounds are drawn as published results; they rely on large computations and are not kernel-checked here. Only Lambda ≤ 1/2, Lambda ≤ 9/32 and Lambda ≥ 0 are kernel-checked in this registry: the machine-checked picture is 0 ≤ Lambda ≤ 9/32. Every plot on this tab is a float model, and the moving zero in step b is a toy model of the bound, not a zero of zeta. conjecture1_proved = False.
a

From a zero-free half-plane to Lambda

A zero rho = beta + i gamma of zeta corresponds to the zero z = 2 gamma - 2 i (beta - 1/2) of H_0, so Im z = -2 (beta - 1/2). If zeta has no zero with Re s > theta, the functional equation also clears Re s < 1 - theta, and every zero of H_0 satisfies |Im z| ≤ Delta = 2 (theta - 1/2). De Bruijn's flow, proved on the dbn island as dbn_debruijn_parametric, says that if every zero of H_t0 has (Im z)^2 ≤ Y, then every zero of H_t with t ≥ t0 has (Im z)^2 ≤ max(Y - 2 (t - t0), 0). Starting from Y = Delta^2 at t0 = 0, the zeros are real once t ≥ Delta^2 / 2 = 2 (theta - 1/2)^2.

OpenAI's quasi-Riemann hypothesis (zeta(s) ≠ 0 for Re s > 7/8) gives theta = 7/8, so Lambda ≤ 2 (3/8)^2 = 9/32. Move the slider to see what any other half-plane would buy.

How high would we need to look

The other way to bound Lambda is the Polymath15 criterion: rule out zeros of H_t0 up to a height X by computation, and above it by an analytic bound. Our scoping run evaluated that criterion at heights a kernel could plausibly check. The orange points are its smallest certifiable Lambda at each barrier height; they are floating numerics (IEEE double plus mpmath), not Arb-rigorous and not kernel-checked. At X/2 = 10^4, 10^5, 6.4 x 10^5 it reaches only about 0.678, 0.510, 0.429, all worse than 9/32. The fitted curve is Lambda ~ 5.06 / log(X / 4 pi) - 0.012.

The reason is the 1 / log X barrier (Polymath15, section 10). A zero off the line at height T is pushed toward the axis by its neighbours at a speed of about log T, so it needs a time of about 1 / log T to arrive. A criterion that sees only up to height X cannot exclude such a zero just above X at an earlier time, so it cannot certify much below c / log X. A half-plane is different: it holds at every height at once, which is why the 7/8 half-plane beats every kernel-feasible height on this plot. A half-plane at theta beats a height-X point exactly when 2 (theta - 1/2)^2 is below it.

Riemann's missing surface

The short version

We know why the gamma factor and the Euler product fit together. Tate's thesis makes each local factor the Mellin transform of a self-dual function at its place, glued by one global Poisson summation. What is missing is the geometry in which that fit becomes a positive pairing, the analogue of the Hodge index theorem on a curve times itself. Every probe we ran points to the same place, and three results make it precise.

  • The missing piece is archimedean. Every local term of Weil's form is a positive transverse weight off the diagonal plus a finite part on it, at every prime and at infinity. All the negativity at infinity sits in one diagonal constant. All the growth of the weighted genus comes from the gamma factor.
  • Existing geometry is quantised. For every maximal parabolic of a split group, the Eisenstein constant term reaches ζ through the trivial, non-tempered representation, with a shift of exactly 1 between the two ξ arguments. Positivity at any smaller shift is equivalent to a zero-free half-plane, so no existing structure can supply it.
  • Weil's proof breaks at one step. Transported line by line, it first fails at the Hodge index. On the available span that step is RH itself, but it splits into a half we have and a missing Riemann-Roch identity on a constructed square: Conjecture RR-□.

How far we have come

We have not proved RH, and nothing built here is close to a proof. What exists is a sizeable body of kernel-checked results around RH, a precise Lean statement of what is still open, and a verification pipeline that has held up under independent checks. All of it is verification, formalisation and infrastructure, not movement on RH.

  • ProvedWeil positivity for every test function up to support half-width 1/2, by kernel certificate (KWin2), and Connes-Consani's archimedean positivity proved their own conceptual way (Theorem 1, Theorem 4.7, Corollary 2.3; about 24,500 Lean lines, PR #699).

  • ProvedRH stated exactly as what remains. With e9's pole-free converse, a Lean theorem says RH holds if and only if Weil positivity holds for every pole-free test function whose support is not inside [−0.5493, 0.5493]. A reformulation of the open problem, not progress on it.

Ruled out, each with evidence: pushing the zero-free half-plane materially further with OpenAI's method (7/8 is a two-sided vertex at ℓ = 1/6; Liu's 0.874957 is the optimum at κ = 3/4, and freeing κ buys about 5×10−8, Mori, unverified; moving the constant by more than hairs needs a different symbol order, and that is blocked: the sextic bottleneck); reaching Λ ≤ 0.22 by this route (it needs zero computations beyond what the kernel can check); getting RH from finite windows (the floor decays like exp(−(60…80)L²), and Davenport-Heilbronn, which has off-line zeros, passes every rung up to half-width about 1.713); a structural proof of the pole step even with one prime (its margin falls to about 4×10−8); and the candidate structures in the gates below.

The honest bottom line. The work has moved from "here are some bounds" to an exact Lean statement of what RH requires, on a small, well-tested foundation. The remaining step is the open core of the problem.

The d-ladder

For a shift 0 < d ≤ 1, put σ = (1+d)/2. By the functional equation, the ratio below has modulus 1, and its time delay is a single real function.

ξ((1−d)/2 + it) / ξ(σ + it) = conj ξ(σ+it) / ξ(σ+it),  τd(t) = 2 Re ξ′/ξ(σ + it)

Shift 1 is Eisenstein scattering on the modular surface. As d → 0, τd/d tends to Laguerre's −(log Ξ)″. Positivity at every shift is RH (Lagarias).

  • ProvedPositivity is innerness. Every rung has modulus 1, so modulus carries no sign. The sign that matters is innerness: ξ(w−d)/ξ(w) bounded by 1 to the right of the line, which holds exactly when ζ has no zeros there. Equivalently, τd′ > 0 at all heights for every shift d′ ≥ d. Positivity on one line alone is weaker: in function-field tests a zero can sit to the right of the line while the delay stays positive. de Branges spaces and Lax-Phillips systems below shift 1 exist exactly under innerness, so they record a zero-free region and cannot produce one.
  • ProvedTheorem Q, quantisation. For every maximal parabolic of a split group, with trivial data and the spherical vector, the constant term on the unitary axis reduces to strings ξ(cs+a)/ξ(cs+1−a) with a a half-integer, a ≤ 0. So the shift is a whole number d ≥ 1. The 1 is the degree of Haar measure on the unipotent radical. Proved in that setting; the GLn residual case with ζ-data is contained in it, and the version with cuspidal data, where the factors are Rankin-Selberg L-functions rather than ξ, is only sketched. Quasi-split groups give Hecke L-functions, not ζ. Conjecturedin general, following from Arthur's conjecture.
  • ComputedExact arithmetic on 33 distinct split cases, every maximal parabolic of A1 to A6, B2, B3, C3, D4 and G2, plus 23 rescaled cases for the cubic cover, gives only whole-number shifts; the check uses the proof's own Gindikin-Karpelevich formula, so it confirms the algebra, not the normalisation. For the cubic cover the normalizer ζ(1+6s)/ζ(2+6s) is kernel-checked in Lean in AxiomMath's library.
  • ProvedContinuous shifts exist only without a sign. Rankin-Selberg periods move ζ's arguments continuously, but on the spectral axis they enter only as moduli. A family built from non-unitary representations reaches every shift, but its only invariant form is indefinite.

The genus moves with the scale

Bombieri and Stepanov proved RH for curves without a surface, from Riemann-Roch, the vanishing of Frobenius and amplification. Transcribed over ℤ, that route needs a Riemann-Roch theorem filtered by scale whose genus grows slower than exponentially in L = log x.

  • ProvedOn a curve, the genus is the rank deficiency of the restricted Weil form, once the window passes g log q (checked on 109 curves).
  • ComputedA weighted trace dimension of the window form reproduces Schoenfeld's weighted genus Σ 1/|ρ| = (1/2π) log²(T/2π) − 0.0345. All its log² growth comes from the archimedean place; the finite places contribute only a conductor term linear in log T.
  • ProvedFor ζ, degree and genus grow at the same scale, so the regime "beyond g log q" that makes the curve proof work never arrives. Trace-type dimensions carry no sign. The sign would be the positive-definiteness of the scaling flow's spectral measure, which no trace can see.

Where Weil's proof breaks

Weil proved RH for a curve C over a finite field with the Hodge index theorem on C × C, applied to the graphs of Frobenius. We transported that proof to Spec ℤ step by step.

  • ProvedOn curves the dictionary is exact. Our restricted Weil form is minus the intersection form on the Frobenius graphs, restricted to the part orthogonal to the fibres. The fibre classes are the pole terms, the diagonal's self-intersection is the genus term, and the Lefschetz formula is the explicit formula. Castelnuovo-Severi is Weil positivity. Checked exactly on 20 curves.
  • ProvedThe sign has one source: the ampleness of the two fibre classes, carried into positivity by Riemann-Roch on the surface. Neither the product structure nor the finite Néron-Severi rank produces it.
  • ProvedOver ℚ, the first step that fails is the Hodge index. The earlier steps carry over on the span of the scaling graphs and the fibres, with the pairing taken from the explicit formula. On that span the Hodge index is Weil's criterion, and so is any Riemann-Roch inequality stated only there.
  • ProvedThe failure splits in two. The available half is positivity on effective combinations of graphs: the local weights are positive at every place, this is where the Euler product enters, and Davenport-Heilbronn fails it. The missing half is a Riemann-Roch index identity on a constructed surface.

Conjecture RR-□ (a sharpened form of Connes's programme). There is a square of Spec ℤ, built without reference to the zeros of ζ, with divisors containing the scaling graphs, principal divisors, and section spaces whose dimensions are real numbers of von Neumann type. A divisor with sections must be equivalent to an effective one, effective combinations of graphs must have positive degree, and sections must have no zero divisors (a degree bound also works). In characteristic one that last condition is automatic, which leaves the surface, its Riemann-Roch identity and the positivity of graphs as the open content. And for every window there is an index identity χ(nD) = ½n² D·D + O(n), with Serre duality, whose leading term is Weil's form.

ProvedThis implies Weil positivity on each window, and windows covering every pole-free test function imply RH. Both steps are kernel-checked in Lean and merged into Arda (PRs #711 and #712), with Weil positivity derived from the axioms rather than assumed. Since such windows exist trivially if RH holds, the open content is the construction. The sign comes from h¹ ≥ 0 together with the absence of zero divisors; apart from those two inequalities, the missing statement is an identity. OpenIt is not known to be weaker than RH. Gate 5's genus obstruction does not block it, because n acts as a degree independent of the scale.

  • ProvedThe analytic families sit downstream of the sign. The de Branges and ladder forms equal Weil's form on damped test functions, so the Hodge index implies their positivity, not the reverse. Their limit as the shift goes to 0 sees only the zeros on the line. The only flow that pushes positivity downward is de Bruijn's heat flow, already used for Λ ≤ 0.2812.
  • ProvedRR-□ is now a kernel-checked Lean statement (e9 session; 13 theorems, standard axioms only, merged to Arda as PR #711 under telperion/examples/weil_form_enclosure; not independently reviewed). The axioms imply Weil positivity, stated with Arda's own Weil-form definitions. They hold for a genus-2 curve over F₅ and fail for an off-circle fake. Stating it in Lean caught a gap: without the degree bound, a three-dimensional model satisfies the other axioms and violates the Hodge index. A follow-up removed the tension this seemed to create. The Lean work now shows that "no zero divisors" alone suffices, and in Connes and Consani's characteristic-one framework sections are tropical functions whose product is a sum, so they have no zero divisors automatically. Checked on their periodic orbits, not yet on a square; the dimension-theory step is Engelking's subspace theorem. A fake with negative point counts also satisfies the axioms, so positivity of graphs needs its own axiom.

Tests any candidate must pass

  1. Euler product. It must use Hecke multiplicativity, so that Davenport-Heilbronn falls out at construction time.
  2. Eigenvalue positions. In its function-field version, its sign must depend on where the Frobenius eigenvalues sit, not only on how many there are.
  3. Existence without zeros. Its positivity must be proved without reference to the zeros. Otherwise it records a zero-free region rather than producing one.
  4. Shift below 1. Its time delay must be τd for some d < 1 at every height, which no constant term can give.
  5. Genus. Its degree must be separable from the scale, with a genus that grows slower than exponentially in log x. A weighted archimedean dimension of (1/2π) log²(T/2π) is necessary.

What new mathematics must supply. A definite Hermitian structure on an infinite-dimensional space carrying the scaling flow. Its time delay realises a shift below 1, equivalently a unipotent-type object of non-integral Haar degree. It is Hecke-stable, its existence is proved without reference to zeros, it passes the positions filter, and its degree is separable from the scale.

Nothing in reductive groups, their covers, Eisenstein series, Rankin-Selberg periods, complementary series, de Branges spaces or Lax-Phillips systems has these properties.

Open leads

  • CheckedConnes and Consani's square fails RR-□ on content. Only the formal axioms hold. It has no divisors containing the graphs, no principal divisors, no section spaces and no Riemann-Roch; its pairing equals Weil's form because it is defined from the trace formula. Every intersection of two graphs there is an excess intersection along the archimedean locus, so deriving the pairing geometrically needs a regularised excess-intersection theory. The most tractable missing piece is a two-dimensional Riemann-Roch on the square of a single periodic orbit (Lemma S), now with the e9 session. Its first step showed that all the graphs meet at one cone point. Its second proved that on the compact single-orbit square the graphs cannot be Cartier divisors at all: going once around a loop rescales a graph's crease by 1/p, which forces it to vanish. So the pairings computed earlier were between classes that do not exist as divisors there, and only the fibres survive, giving a form blind to ζ. Graphs become genuine divisors only on a space that is non-compact along the scaling flow, with an end at the archimedean cone point, or after a Frobenius twist; there the multiplier et is exactly the factor p picked up per loop.
  • SpeculativeConjecture RR-□ is the sharpest target. Evidence for its available half: positive local weights at every place, and a natural candidate for the principal divisors, the Weil form's kernel built from Riemann's Φ, which exists with or without RH. Against the missing half: no known square of Spec ℤ has divisors or section spaces, and Bost's theta-type dimension never vanishes, so it cannot serve as the section spaces.
  • ClosedThe last lead, a finite-codimension positive subspace inside the indefinite family that reaches every shift, is closed. The family's own form is defined from the representations alone and is blind to eigenvalues. The negative squares of the relevant kernel count exactly the zeros to the right of the line (Krein-Langer; matched in all 30 test cases). In function fields that count is 0 or infinite, so finite codimension forces codimension 0, which is RH on that line. The Pontryagin relaxation buys nothing. Tested by the e9 session, AI work, not reviewed; Hecke stability not computed.
  • SpeculativeA tropical route that skips Riemann-Roch. Cartwright proved a Hodge index for tropical surfaces with no Riemann-Roch theorem at all, by a local-to-global argument: each vertex star contributes exactly one positive direction, and one global relation cancels all but one. The adelic version would take the places as vertices and the product formula as the relation. Its first test fails for the naive local terms. Each prime's term is positive on a fixed fraction of the frequency circle (a quarter at p = 2) and repeats periodically, so it has infinitely many positive directions. The archimedean term comes closest: it has only about 2L × 6.29 / π positive directions on a window of half-width L, finite but growing. No place has exactly one. Local pieces with that shape would have to come from the geometry of the scaling site itself. Found by the e9 session; not reviewed.
  • ComputedShort windows as the local pieces. Windows of the scaling line of length about ½ do have exactly one positive direction each, once the pole terms are placed correctly. With one pole-type combination per window, the full form already cancels all but one positive direction, through the prime cross-terms, with comfortable margins. What remains is Weil's criterion itself, with its tiny margins. The coupling between windows is the Fourier transform of the zero measure and does not decay with distance, so any genuinely local construction would have to encode the zero sum at every distance. Exact local block lifts do exist on short windows, but their existence is blind to RH: they survive when the form is pushed into violating Weil positivity. All of the sign sits in counting the relations, and that count is Weil's criterion. A natural candidate geometry would make u and u + log p neighbours.
  • ComputedThat candidate also reduces to Weil's criterion. On the complex whose edges join u to u ± k log p, local pieces have one positive direction only when each point carries a single degree of freedom; with richer test functions they overcount, for structural reasons rather than because of the prime terms. With every prime as an edge, the coupling left over is the short-range archimedean tail, which no prime edge reaches; on a lattice built from 2 and 3 alone it is mostly the primes left out. The relations that would cancel the extra directions cannot be principal in the test space, and requiring exactly enough of them is Weil positivity on the window. Davenport-Heilbronn's local pieces are as good as ζ's or better, so local tests are blind to RH. The one local difference, that ζ's prime graph has nonnegative weights while Davenport-Heilbronn's is signed and frustrated, turns out to be the classical positivity of Λ(n): it acts only through the pole direction, as in de la Vallée Poussin's argument at the edge of the strip, and an elliptic-curve L-function that satisfies the generalized RH is just as frustrated. Cross-checked against an independent run by the e9 session, which it reproduces exactly; that run's one-direction-per-point result is automatic when each point carries a single degree of freedom.
  • CheckedTwo more routes closed, and a three-way obstruction. A polarization scaled by the flow, on Deninger's model of Frobenius, turns out to be RR-□'s own conclusion restated, with the Euler product sitting in effectivity rather than positivity. Weil's other proof, through the Jacobian, needs a geometrically preserved nef cone; Connes and Consani's Jacobian of Spec ℤ has none. No existing object combines all three of what is needed: a connected scaling family on which the flow acts by et, a nef cone the geometry preserves, and a non-toric H¹. The excess intersection at the cone point in Lemma S has the first two, so the question there is whether it can carry the third. Found by the Riemann session; not reviewed.
  • CheckedBuilding the geometry directly: not for ζ. Three agents constructed eighteen candidates: foliated spaces in Deninger's style, characteristic-one spaces with an extra H¹, and the automorphic GL2 tower. The three requirements turn out to be easy to meet artificially, so the guards decide. Two candidates came closest. A one-prime foliated space meets all three, but for the zeta function of an elliptic curve over Fp, not for ζ. The Connes-Meyer adele class space has ζ's zeros as its own spectrum and Weil's formula as its own trace formula, but no sign. The failures share one pattern. At a prime, Frobenius acts like √p times a rotation. Every sign mechanism found controls the √p and none controls the rotation, which is where the positions of the zeros live. That is also true of Weil's own proof for curves: there the rotations are pinned not by the sign but by derived intersection numbers, which is exactly what is missing over ℚ. Two results explain part of this. A ζ Euler factor in the cohomology of the modular tower is always of Tate type, so it can appear only in even weight, never in a pure odd-weight H¹; this rules out ζ as Frobenius or Hecke data in H¹, but not a spectral realisation of its zeros. And the positivity condition that matters for scalings of H¹ is conformality for the Hodge metric, while the absence of continuous map-induced scalings comes from discreteness alone; on a sum over primes, independent phases per prime remain allowed. What is missing is a two-leg object over ℚ, the analogue of a curve times itself with two partial Frobenius maps, whose sign constrains the phases. A last observation links back to the start: with every prime present, a connected scaling flow cannot be absorbed by the p-adic directions, so the space must have an end at the archimedean place, and the archimedean term of the trace is exactly the regularisation at that end. The single negative constant found in gate 3 may be the price of that end. Found by this session's agents; not reviewed.
  • ConjecturedTheorem Q beyond degenerate data. A Lean statement for SL2 and GLn would make the barrier kernel-checked.
  • SpeculativeSomething outside reductive groups altogether: a unipotent-type object of non-integral Haar degree, or a Riemann-Roch theorem whose degree is independent of the scale. No construction is known; this is where new mathematics would have to begin.

Gate 11: trying harder at infinity

The independent review reopened two doors, and the two-leg work named the one derived number missing over ℚ, the diagonal's self-intersection. Three agents attacked them in parallel on 13 October. All three fail, and they fail at the same place.

  • ProvedNo derived self-intersection over ℚ. Over ℚ the diagonal's self-intersection is not a number but a distribution, and its mass at the identity u = 1 depends on the regularisation: −(ψ(¼) − log π) = 5.37218 in one natural scheme, γ + log 2π = 2.41509 in another, differing by exactly π/2 + 2 log 2. That mass is invisible to every tangential deformation of the diagonal. In Connes's function-field trace formula the self-intersection enters as an exact lattice-count offset only because the test function can be held constant on a compact open subgroup; the positive reals have none.
  • ComputedMatching the archimedean constant says nothing. ψ(¼) − log π = −2ζ′/ζ(½), to 30 digits, an identity of the functional equation alone. Its function-field analogue holds for a fake zeta function that violates RH, so the constant carries neither a sign nor the positions of the zeros. Two of these numbers were checked independently to 25 digits by the Riemann session.
  • ProvedTheorem N, a flat archimedean leaf. On a compact surface lamination whose flow is conformal on the leaves, a fixed point forces its leaf to be the complex plane, and by Candel's uniformisation theorem (Theorem 4.3) Connes's Euler characteristic is then 0. ConditionalThe first version concluded that Deninger's dictionary, which sets that Euler characteristic to −log|dK|, could hold only for ℚ. An independent review by the Riemann session confirmed the topology but found the arithmetic step a gap: the dictionary's formula is only a heuristic in Deninger's papers, and a short naturality argument forces the Euler characteristic to be 0 for every number field anyway. So the conflict is internal to the dictionary and does not single out ℚ; for every field the discriminant term has to move into the extension at the archimedean point, as it already does for ℚ. The extension to coverings is also a gap. What survives is the topological fact and the conclusion that the archimedean place behaves as an end, where the adele class space is the leaf space of such a system rather than the system itself.
  • ComputedThe twist guard is class field theory. In a foliated model the twist of the prime phases is a flat holonomy around each prime orbit, not a change of the local return maps, which never enter the trace. Random flat twists break the positivity theorem in 400 of 400 samples, so the twists a construction must allow are exactly the Hecke characters.
  • ProvedIndependent phases per prime add nothing. Complex conjugation is a symmetry of ζ's Weil form that flips the sign of every derivative in the prime phases, so positivity is lost at first order in almost every direction. Weil positivity holds at an isolated point of the torus of phases, while per-prime conformality holds everywhere, so the first cannot be assembled from the second. ComputedWith all 25 primes below 100, ζ's point was the only positive one among 3,000 random phase vectors and all 65,536 sign vectors on the primes up to 53; Davenport-Heilbronn is negative.

The sharpened missing piece. A canonical extension, given by the geometry rather than chosen, of the archimedean fixed-point term across u = 1, carrying a sign. Every extension we can choose ourselves restates Weil's criterion. In the surviving Deninger route this is where the archimedean band of negative frequencies, |t| < 6.29, would have to come from.

Gate 12: the extension at u = 1

Gate 11 pointed at one spot: the archimedean term has to be extended across u = 1, and the choice of extension seemed to carry the missing information. Two agents tested whether some principle fixes that extension canonically and with a sign. Neither found one, and together they show it is not a separate ingredient.

  • ProvedThe ambiguity is exactly one constant. Near u = 1 the kernel behaves like 1/(2|x|) + ¼, so the extensions with the same singularity are one fixed extension plus c·δ, and nothing else. No extension is invariant under dilation. Fourier self-duality does not fix c: changing the additive character shifts it by log|a|. The global functional equation fixes c = ψ(¼) − log π, which is the known identity again, and assigns Davenport-Heilbronn and an odd Dirichlet character the same constant. Dimensional regularisation reproduces exactly the other natural value, −(γ + log 2π).
  • ProvedRH does not depend on c. For every value of c, the pole-free Weil form shifted by c·δ is bounded below if and only if RH holds; under RH the bound is 0, so the known constant is exactly the threshold. An independent review by the e9 session confirmed this and removed an extra finiteness hypothesis from the proof, so the statement is unconditional; it is probably known, in the spirit of Yoshida's semiboundedness results, and no novelty is claimed. ComputedThe margins on windows of half-width L are 1.19×10−2 at 0.5, 1.5×10−23 at 1 and at most about 10−64 at 2 (not converged), decaying like exp(−(50 to 65)L²), the same rate as the HODGE-1 prime margin. The constant is saturated, not the source of the sign.
  • ComputedThe prolate operator sees the density of zeros, not their positions. Connes and Moscovici prove their prolate wave operator has a unique self-adjoint extension for each cutoff λ, but that extension lives at x = ±λ, not at u = 1, and log(2/λ²) plays the role of the constant c. Its negative spectrum tracks the squares of ζ's zeros. With the smooth trend removed, its points coincide with Gram points, and its residual against ζ's zeros (0.231 mean spacings) is no better than theirs (0.237); against Davenport-Heilbronn's zeros it is 0.203. Its sign comes from the gamma factor alone.

The next construction, and a test that turned out too weak. The sign must come from the whole form, archimedean and prime terms together, and no single place can supply it, so any candidate has to couple the primes to the archimedean place. We first proposed a numerical bar: after detrending, fit ζ's zeros better than Gram points do (residual below 0.237) while failing on Davenport-Heilbronn. ComputedThe Riemann session showed that known objects already pass it. Gonek's symmetrised Euler product reaches 0.0069 with primes up to 9973, and the 2025 Connes-Consani-Moscovici construction does about a thousand times better than Gram points; both fail on Davenport-Heilbronn only structurally or on a window, which is Weil's criterion again. Positions and sign decouple. The amended test: a candidate must also carry a sign that it derives itself and that does not collapse to Weil's criterion.

Where this leaves us

Twelve gates, three parallel sessions and several dozen research agents have narrowed the missing piece to one object, and every route that avoids building it has been closed with evidence.

  • What is settled. RH is equivalent, in kernel-checked Lean, to constructing Riemann-Roch windows with the RR-□ properties for every pole-free test function, and such windows exist trivially if RH is true. So the open content is a construction, not an inequality.
  • What every probe found. Each sign mechanism we could find, analytic, tropical, automorphic, foliated or at the archimedean end, controls the size of Frobenius and never its phase. The positions of the zeros live in the phases. The archimedean end, the last place a phase-sensitive correction could hide, sees only the twist-invariant part of Weil's form.
  • What would have to be built. A two-leg object over ℚ, the analogue of a curve times itself with two partial Frobenius maps. The Riemann session's TWOLEG effort sharpened what that means. In Weil's proof for curves, the sign constrains only the size of the Frobenius eigenvalues; their phases are fixed by intersection numbers the geometry derives, the fixed-point counts. So the missing object needs genuinely derived intersection numbers, including the diagonal's self-intersection, with the sign acting on sizes. Over ℚ this comes down to an arithmetic analogue of the base point Spec k, over which Spec ℤ × Spec ℤ would be a real product with fibres. None is known; the Witt-vector and Habiro candidates give a ruled surface whose ample class cancels the prime lengths. Gate 11 showed why a derived self-intersection is hard over ℚ: at the archimedean fixed point it is a distribution with no canonical mass. Gate 12 then showed that this extension is a single constant fixed by the functional equation, so the sign has to come from coupling the primes to the archimedean place.
  • What it is not. None of this is progress on RH. It is a precise description of a missing object, built on a small foundation of kernel-checked results.

Compute it yourself

Everything on this page can be regenerated from the repository, and the claims behind it can be re-checked at three levels: the registry gate, the Lean kernel, and an independent second kernel.

The registry gate

The verify gate re-reads every campaign, matches each proved node's verbatim statement against its artifact, scans the artifact for incompleteness markers, and checks that a runnable CI step compiles the artifact's module. It is read-only.

cd telperion
python -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -e . pyyaml
.venv/bin/telperion mission status mirrormere
.venv/bin/telperion mission verify mirrormere
.venv/bin/telperion mission verify            # every campaign

Do not run mission grant or mission audit to reproduce this page; they mutate the registry. The page is built from the same loader the gate uses.

Build an island

Each example island is a self-contained Lake project with its own toolchain pin. The quasicrystal island and the rvm_bridge island are on v4.32.0; the li_positivity island is on v4.34.0-rc1. A full Mathlib build is required the first time.

cd telperion/examples/rvm_bridge/lean
lake exe cache get      # Mathlib oleans
lake build              # every module in the lakefile's defaultTargets
lake env lean AxiomGuardRvMBridge.lean   # prints #print axioms for each node theorem

The axiom guard is what the phrase "3-axiom clean" means: each theorem prints exactly [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]. A sorryAx, a smuggled axiom, or native_decide's Lean.ofReduceBool fails the guard.

The Comparator

The Comparator (leanprover/comparator, from OpenAI's ten-proofs) exports a challenge module and a solution module with lean4export and asserts statement identity, an axiom whitelist, and a kernel replay in a second kernel (nanoda). The repository wires it as a CI job for the R3Cert proofs and for the emitter examples; telperion/docs/COMPARATOR.md is the reference.

import mpmath as mp
mp.mp.dps = 30
gam = [mp.im(mp.zetazero(k)) for k in range(1, 201)]        # ordinates (float model)
def F(c, lam):                                              # the Gaussian-derivative face
    return sum(2 * (g - c)**2 * mp.e**(-2 * lam * (g - c)**2) for g in gam)
def li(N):                                                  # paired zero sum, no tail
    return sum(2 * (1 - mp.cos(N * 2 * mp.atan(1 / (2 * g)))) for g in gam)
