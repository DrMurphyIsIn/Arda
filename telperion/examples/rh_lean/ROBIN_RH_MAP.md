# The Robin -> RH Terrain: a sourced map (D1)

> **This does NOT prove RH and does not narrow the gap to it.** "Robin's inequality for all n >= 5041" IS the Riemann Hypothesis (Robin's equivalence). Everything below is either a finite check, an unconditional partial result on a restricted family, or an equivalent reformulation that *relocates* RH's difficulty without reducing it. `conjecture1_proved` stays False.

Generated from a source-grounded literature dive (4 clusters, 46 extracted theorems). Notation: sigma(n) = sum of divisors; G(n) = sigma(n)/(n log log n); gamma = Euler-Mascheroni; e^gamma ~ 1.7810724.


## Robin/Gronwall + the wall

- **Gronwall (1913): limsup of the normalized divisor sum** *(Gronwall 1913 (T. H. Gronwall, Trans. AMS 14, 1913); stated in Zimov 2025, arXiv:2510.23889v1, Sec. 1)* [elementary]  
  limsup_{n->infinity} sigma(n)/(n log log n) = e^gamma, where sigma is the sum-of-divisors function and gamma is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. Equivalently, with G(n) := sigma(n)/(n log log n), limsup G(n) = e^gamma. (Cited as background; Zimov states Robin's inequality G(n) < e^gamma is the n>5040 refinement of the fact that e^gamma is the limsup.)  
  _Proof idea:_ Mertens' third theorem: for n with the extremal (primorial-like / CA) factorization, sigma(n)/n ~ prod_{p|n}(1-1/p)^{-1} ~ e^gamma log log n; the extremal densest-prime configuration attains e^gamma in the limsup while all n stay below it in the limit sup sense.
- **Robin (1984) equivalence** *(G. Robin 1984, 'Grandes valeurs de la fonction somme des diviseurs et hypothese de Riemann', J. Math. Pures Appl. 63, 187-213; stated in Zimov 2025 arXiv:2510.23889v1 Sec.1 [7])* [deep/analytic]  
  RH is true if and only if G(n) = sigma(n)/(n log log n) < e^gamma for all integers n > 5040 (equivalently sigma(n) < e^gamma n log log n for all n >= 5041). The number 5040 is the largest known exception; RH true => no exception beyond it.  
  _Proof idea:_ Robin relates the excess of sigma(n)/n over e^gamma log log n to an explicit sum over zeta zeros (via Chebyshev psi / Mertens estimates); a zero off the critical line forces the inequality to fail infinitely often, while RH forces the error term small enough that 5040 is the last exception. Uses zero-free-region / explicit-formula analytic number theory.
- **Robin's conditional lower bound if RH false (Robin 1984, Prop. 1 Sec. 4)** *(Robin 1984, Prop. 1 of Sec. 4; restated as Broughan 2017 Lemma 7.15 and Lagarias 2002 Thm 3.2; Zimov 2025 arXiv:2510.23889v1 Thm 7)* [deep/analytic]  
  Assume RH is false. Let theta = sup{ Re(rho) : zeta(rho)=0, rho nontrivial }, so theta > 1/2. Then for any b in (1-theta, 1/2) there exists a constant c>0 such that G(n) > e^gamma (1 + c/(log n)^b) for INFINITELY MANY n. (This is the 'higher threshold' / infinite set.)  
  _Proof idea:_ A zeta zero with real part theto>1/2 injects an oscillating term of size x^{theta-1} into the error of the Chebyshev/psi estimate feeding sigma(n)/n; choosing n along an extremal (CA) sequence at the peaks of that oscillation makes the excess over e^gamma log log n as large as e^gamma * c/(log n)^b infinitely often. b<theta-... constrained by b<1/2 since theta could approach 1.
- **Akbary-Friggstad (2009): least counterexample is superabundant** *(A. Akbary and Z. Friggstad 2009, Amer. Math. Monthly 116, 273-275, Theorem 3; Zimov 2025 arXiv:2510.23889v1 Thm 3)* [elementary]  
  The smallest integer n>5040 (if any) that fails Robin's inequality G(n)<e^gamma must be a superabundant number (a number with sigma(n)/n strictly larger than sigma(k)/k for all k<n).  
  _Proof idea:_ If n fails Robin's inequality but is not superabundant, some smaller m<n has sigma(m)/m >= sigma(n)/n; since log log is increasing, m also fails, contradicting minimality of n.
- **Broughan (2017): least counterexample can be taken CA** *(K. A. Broughan, 'Equivalents of the Riemann Hypothesis, Vol. I', Cambridge Univ. Press 2017, Lemma 7.1; Zimov 2025 arXiv:2510.23889v1 Thm 1)* [elementary]  
  If a counterexample to Robin's inequality exists for some n>5040, then there exists a counterexample which is a colossally abundant (CA) number. (Contrapositive: if no CA number >5040 fails Robin's inequality, RH holds.)  
  _Proof idea:_ CA numbers are the extremizers of sigma(k)/k^{1+eps}; any superabundant counterexample can be pushed to a nearby CA number without decreasing the normalized ratio enough to satisfy the inequality.
- **Consecutive-CA quotient structure (Broughan 2017 Lemma 6.15)** *(Broughan 2017, Lemma 6.15; Zimov 2025 arXiv:2510.23889v1 Thm 2)* [elementary]  
  The quotient n/m of two consecutive CA numbers (m<n) is either a prime p or a product of two distinct primes pq (a semiprime).  
  _Proof idea:_ CA numbers arise from a single ordering of the 'benefit' thresholds log(1+1/(p^{a+1}+...+1))/log p; at a threshold crossing one prime power is incremented, and coincidences (ties) allow at most two distinct primes to increment simultaneously.
- **Alaoglu-Erdos (1944): largest prime factor ~ log n** *(L. Alaoglu and P. Erdos 1944, Trans. AMS 56, 448-469, Theorems 7 and 8; Zimov 2025 arXiv:2510.23889v1 Thms 5,6)* [elementary]  
  The largest prime factor p of a superabundant (hence CA) number n satisfies p ~ log n as n->infinity. Also the quotient of two consecutive superabundant numbers tends to 1.  
  _Proof idea:_ Optimal exponent allocation in a superabundant number forces the largest prime to sit near the point where adding a new prime factor stops paying off, which is at p ~ log n by the prime number theorem / Mertens estimates.
- **Morrill-Platt (2018): Robin verified in a large range** *(T. Morrill and D. Platt 2018, 'Robin's inequality for 25-free integers', arXiv:1809.10813v4, Theorem 5; Zimov 2025 arXiv:2510.23889v1 Thm 4)* [elementary]  
  Robin's inequality G(n)<e^gamma holds for all n with 5040 < n <= 10^(10^13.099) (via reduction to 25-free integers).  
  _Proof idea:_ Reduce to checking 'q-free' / smooth candidate structures and combine explicit primorial computations with analytic bounds to sweep the finite range up to the stated enormous bound.
- **Zimov (2025) main theorem: least CA counterexample confined to a band** *(B. Zimov 2025, 'On the Least Colossally Abundant Exception to Robin's Inequality', arXiv:2510.23889v1, Theorem 8 (with Lemmas 1-5, Thms 1-7))* [elementary]  
  If RH is false, the least CA counterexample n (consecutive CA pair m<n with G(m)<e^gamma and G(n)>=e^gamma) is constrained to the band e^gamma < G(n) < e^gamma (1 + c/(log n)^b), with 0<b<1/2. In particular n is EXCLUDED from Robin's infinite set of Theorem 7 which lies ABOVE the higher threshold e^gamma(1+c/(log n)^b).  
  _Proof idea:_ Compute G(n)/G(m) = (sigma(n)m/(sigma(m)n)) * (loglog m/loglog n). By Lemmas 4-5 the abundancy ratio for a single/double NEW prime is 1+O(1/p), existing-prime increments give 1+O(1/(p sigma(p^a)))=1+O(1/(log n)^2) (faster decay). Since p ~ log n (Thm 5) and loglog m/loglog n = 1+o(1) (Lemma 1), G(n)/G(m) = 1+O(1/log n). With G(m)<e^gamma this yields G(n) < e^gamma(1 + C1/log n). If n also satisfied the Thm-7 lower bound e^gamma(1+c/(log n)^b), then c/(log n)^b < C1/log n would be needed, but their ratio ~ (c/C1)(log n)^{1-b} -> infinity since 1-b>1/2. Contradiction; so the least CA counterexample cannot be in the infinite (above-threshold) set, hence sits in the band below it.

_Takeaways:_ Chain of reduction: Gronwall (limsup G = e^gamma, unconditional, elementary) -> Robin 1984 (RH <=> G(n)<e^gamma for all n>5040, NON-elementary, zeta-zeros) -> Akbary-Friggstad + Broughan (least counterexample is superabundant and can be taken CA, elementary) => RH is equivalent to the asymptotic behavior of G restricted to the sparse CA sequence. Robin's own Prop.1/Sec.4 (Thm 7) supplies the RH-false dichotomy: infinitely many G(n) > e^gamma(1+c/(log n)^b), 0<b<1/2, with b tied to theta=sup Re(zeta zero) and thus to the zero-free region - this IS the wall. Zimov 2025 (arXiv:2510.23889) contributes an ELEMENTARY local-structure computation: consecutive-CA quotients are prime/semiprime (Broughan), largest prime factor p~log n (Alaoglu-Erdos), so a single CA step moves G by only 1+O(1/log n); this forces any least CA counterexample into the band e^gamma < G(n) < e^gamma(1+c/(log n)^b), excluding it from Robin's above-threshold infinite set. IMPORTANT: this is a location constraint, not a resolution - the band is non-empty and RH-false remains consistent, so no RH proof. Most sub-results (Gronwall, Alaoglu-Erdos, Akbary-Friggstad, Broughan lemmas, Zimov's Lemmas 1-5 and Thm 8) are elementary; the load-bearing NON-elementary analytic inputs are Robin's original equivalence (Thm 2) and Robin's conditional lower bound (Thm 7), both of which require zeta-zero / explicit-formula machinery.

## SA/CA reduction, gap lemma, computations

- **Robin criterion (RH equivalence)** *(Robin 1984, J. Math. Pures Appl. 63 (1984) 187-213, Theoreme 1; restated Axler 2021 arXiv:2110.13478 Prop 1.1; Nazardonyavi-Yakubovich arXiv:1211.2147 (1.1))* [deep/analytic]  
  RH is true iff sigma(n) < e^gamma * n * log log n for every integer n >= 5041 (equivalently, for all n > 5040), where sigma is the sum-of-divisors function and gamma = 0.5772... is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. Equivalent form: f(n) := sigma(n)/(n log log n) < e^gamma for all n > 5040. n=5040 is the last known integer where the inequality fails (f(5040) ~ 1.7909).  
  _Proof idea:_ Robin sharpened Ramanujan's conditional bound; under RH the excess sigma(n)/n - e^gamma log log n is O(-sqrt(log n)) with an explicit constant, forcing the inequality for large n, and if RH fails an infinite family of counterexamples must occur (via oscillation of ϑ, Landau/Littlewood). Ties the extremal order lim sup f = e^gamma (Gronwall) to zero-free region of zeta.
- **Gronwall maximal order** *(Gronwall 1913, Trans. AMS 14, 113-122; cited Axler 2021 arXiv:2110.13478 (1.1); Nazar arXiv:1211.2147 (1.3))* [elementary]  
  lim sup_{n->infinity} sigma(n)/(n log log n) = e^gamma. Equivalently lim sup f(n) = e^gamma.  
  _Proof idea:_ From Mertens' theorem prod_{p<=x} p/(p-1) ~ e^gamma log x combined with the fact that sigma(n)/n <= prod_{p|n} p/(p-1) and this is approached along primorials/superabundant numbers.
- **SA reduction of least counterexample** *(Akbary-Friggstad 2009 (Superabundant numbers and the Riemann hypothesis), quoted as Theorem 2 in Nazardonyavi-Yakubovich arXiv:1211.2147 / JIS Vol.17 Nazar4)* [elementary]  
  If there is any counterexample to Robin's inequality (1.3), then the least such counterexample is a superabundant number, where n is superabundant (SA) iff sigma(n)/n > sigma(m)/m for all m < n.  
  _Proof idea:_ If the least counterexample n0 were not SA there is m<n0 with sigma(m)/m >= sigma(n0)/n0; since log log is increasing, m would also violate Robin's inequality, contradicting minimality of n0. Elementary monotonicity argument.
- **Colossally abundant (CA) definition and inclusion CA subset SA** *(Alaoglu-Erdos 1944, Trans. AMS 56, 448-469; Ramanujan 1915/1997; stated Nazar JIS Vol.17 (4) and arXiv:1211.2147, Axler arXiv:2110.13478 sec.2)* [elementary]  
  n is colossally abundant (CA) if for some epsilon>0: sigma(n)/n^{1+epsilon} >= sigma(m)/m^{1+epsilon} for all m<n and > for all m>n. (Equivalently for all integers k>=2, sigma(n)/n^{1+eps} >= sigma(k)/k^{1+eps}.) Every CA number is superabundant: CA subset SA. CA numbers are the s=1 case of Ramanujan's generalized superior highly composite numbers.  
  _Proof idea:_ CA maximizes sigma(n)/n^{1+eps} globally, which is a stronger condition than the local SA maximization of sigma(n)/n; hence CA subset SA. Definition due to Alaoglu-Erdos (1944) / Ramanujan.
- **Consecutive-CA gap lemma (Robin's Proposition 1)** *(Robin 1984 Proposition 1 (J. Math. Pures Appl. 63); gap-lemma form Axler arXiv:2110.13478 sec.2; strict version Nazardonyavi-Yakubovich arXiv:1211.2147 Prop 20 / JIS Vol.17 Nazar4 Prop 20)* [elementary]  
  Robin's maximum-order proposition: if 3 <= N < n < N' where N and N' are two successive (consecutive) CA numbers, then f(n) <= max{f(N), f(N')}. Consequence (gap lemma): if M1 < M2 are consecutive CA numbers both satisfying Robin's inequality (1.3), then Robin's inequality holds for every integer n with M1 <= n <= M2. Nazardonyavi-Yakubovich sharpen the interior bound to a strict inequality: for 3 <= N < n < N' consecutive CA, f(n) < max{f(N), f(N')} (Proposition 20).  
  _Proof idea:_ On the interval between consecutive CA numbers, e^{-eps t} sigma-ratio structure forces f to be dominated by its endpoint CA values because t -> eps*t - log log t is (strictly) convex; Robin's argument gives <=, strict convexity upgrades to < (Nazar Prop 20). So a counterexample, if any, must itself be a CA number, and verifying Robin at consecutive CA endpoints certifies the whole gap.
- **CA generation recipe (epsilon-sweep / x-parameter algorithm)** *(Alaoglu-Erdos 1944; Erdos-Nicolas 1975; Robin 1984; algorithm as stated in Nazardonyavi-Yakubovich JIS Vol.17 Nazar4 sec.4.2 eqns (19)-(21) and arXiv:1211.2147)* [elementary]  
  Define F(x,k) = log(1 + 1/(x + x^2 + ... + x^k)) / log x. For eps>0 let x_1(eps) solve F(x_1,1)=eps and x_k(eps) solve F(x_k,k)=eps for k>1. Let E_p = {F(p,alpha): alpha>=1} for prime p and E = union_p E_p. If eps not in E, sigma(n)/n^{1+eps} is maximized at a unique N_eps with prime factorization N_eps = prod_p p^{alpha_p(eps)}, where alpha_p(eps) = floor( log((p^{1+eps}-1)/(p^{eps}-1)) / log p ) - 1, equivalently alpha_p(eps) = k if x_{k+1} < p < x_k (k>=1) and 0 if p > x = x_1. If eps in E, there are 2 or 4 CA numbers of parameter eps given by N_eps = prod_{k=1}^{K} prod_{p<x_k or p<=x_k} p, K the largest integer with x_K >= 2. The largest prime factor p(N) of the largest CA number of parameter eps satisfies F(p,1)=eps => p(N)=p. Sweeping eps downward from large to 0 (crossing the discrete critical values in E, at each of which one prime's exponent increments) enumerates all CA numbers in increasing order.  
  _Proof idea:_ Maximizing prod_p (sigma(p^{a_p})/p^{a_p(1+eps)}) separates over primes; for each prime the optimal exponent is the largest a with the marginal benefit F(p,a) >= eps, giving the floor formula. Critical eps values (set E) are exactly where an exponent jumps, so decreasing eps past each element of E multiplies N by one prime. Alaoglu-Erdos / Erdos-Nicolas / Robin construction.
- **Sole-Planat Psi_t reduction to primorials** *(Sole-Planat 2012 (Integers 12, A?); used by Axler 2021 arXiv:2110.13478 Lemmas 2.1-2.4, Thm 1.2 (21-free))* [elementary]  
  For integer t>=2 define Psi_t(n) = n * prod_{p|n} (1 + 1/p + ... + 1/p^{t-1}). If n is t-free then sigma(n) <= Psi_t(n). With R_t(n) = Psi_t(n)/(n log log n): for k>=2 and N_k <= n < N_{k+1} (N_k = k-th primorial = prod of first k primes), R_t(n) <= R_t(N_k). Also R_t(N_k) = [prod_{p>p_k}(1-p^{-t})^{-1} / (zeta(t) log ϑ(p_k))] * prod_{p<=p_k} p/(p-1), and lim_{k->inf} R_t(N_k) = e^gamma/zeta(t) < e^gamma. Hence for each t, Robin holds for all t-free n >= some N_{k0(t)}.  
  _Proof idea:_ Champions of x->Psi_t(x)/x are the primorials and their multiples (Sole-Planat Prop 1), so maximizing R_t reduces to primorial values; the closed form follows from Euler product for zeta and Mertens; the limit e^gamma/zeta(t) is strictly below e^gamma, leaving only finitely many primorials to check computationally.
- **t-free / t-full progress (Robin unconditional cases)** *(Axler 2021 arXiv:2110.13478 Thms 1.2, 1.4, Cor 2.5; Choie-Lichiardopol-Moree-Sole 2007; Sole-Planat 2012; Broughan-Trudgian 2015; Morrill-Platt 2021 (Integers 21, A28))* [elementary]  
  Robin's inequality holds for every t-free integer n>=5041 for progressively larger t: 5-free (Choie et al. 2007), 7-free (Sole-Planat 2012), 11-free (Broughan-Trudgian 2015), 20-free (Morrill-Platt 2021), 21-free (Axler 2021, Thm 1.2). Corollary (Axler 2.5): RH is true iff Robin's inequality holds for all 21-full integers. Axler Thm 1.4 also proves Robin for n>=5041 whenever nu_2(n)<=20, or nu_5(n)<=8, or nu_p(n)<=4 for 11<p<=19, <=3 for 19<p<=41, <=2 for 41<p<=139, =1 for 139<p<=1777.  
  _Proof idea:_ Bound sigma(n) by Psi_t(n) on t-free n, reduce to primorials (R_t <= R_t(N_k)), and use effective Chebyshev ϑ / prime-product estimates plus the Morrill-Platt computational base case to push R_t(N_k) < e^gamma for all k.
- **Robin unconditional weak bound** *(Robin 1984 Theoreme 2 (constant 0.648214); Axler 2021 arXiv:2110.13478 (1.5), Thm 1.3)* [elementary]  
  Unconditionally, for every integer n>=3: sigma(n)/n < e^gamma log log n * (1 + 0.6483/(log log n)^2), equivalently f(n) <= e^gamma + 0.648214/(log log n)^2 with the constant approximately (7/3 - e^gamma log log 12) log log 12, maximal at n=12. Axler Thm 1.3 sharpens: for n not in A = {1,2,4,5,6,8,9,10,12,16,18,20,24,30,36,48,60,72,84,120,180,240,360,840,2520,5040}, sigma(n)/n < e^gamma log log n (1 + 0.0094243/(log log n)^3).  
  _Proof idea:_ Uses a lower bound for Chebyshev's ϑ-function and sigma(n)/n < n/phi(n) = prod_{p|n}(1-1/p)^{-1}, combined with effective ϑ estimates (Broadbent-Kadiri-Lumley-Ng-Wilk 2021) to control the primorial maximum, then finite check of the exceptional set.
- **Computational frontier: Briggs and Morrill-Platt bounds** *(Briggs 2006, Experiment. Math. 15, 251-256; Morrill-Platt 2021, Integers 21 A28; both quoted in Axler 2021 arXiv:2110.13478 sec.2 & Lemma 2.3)* [elementary]  
  Briggs (2006) computed that Robin's inequality holds for every CA number n with 5041 <= n <= 10^(10^10); by the consecutive-CA gap lemma it therefore holds for every integer n with 5041 <= n <= 10^(10^10). Morrill-Platt (2021) extended this to every integer n with 5041 <= n <= 10^(10^13.11485), using 100-bit interval arithmetic. The sharper form (Axler Lemma 2.3, from Morrill-Platt): with k0 = 999,999,476,056, p_{k0} = 29,996,208,012,611, Robin's inequality holds for every integer n with 5041 <= n <= N_{k0} (the k0-th primorial).  
  _Proof idea:_ Enumerate all CA numbers up to the target via the epsilon-sweep, evaluate f at each with rigorous (100-bit interval) arithmetic to certify f < e^gamma at each CA number, then invoke Robin's gap lemma to cover every integer in each consecutive-CA interval. Purely computational + the gap lemma; no new analytic content.
- **Extremely abundant (XA) numbers and RH equivalence** *(Nazardonyavi-Yakubovich arXiv:1211.2147 Thms 6,7,23, Lemma 21 / JIS Vol.17 Nazar4)* [deep/analytic]  
  Define f as in (2). A number n>=10080 is extremely abundant (XA) if f(n) >= f(m) for all m with 10080 <= m < n (the initial XA number is 10080). Theorem 7: RH is true iff there are infinitely many XA numbers (#XA = infinity). Theorem 6: if any counterexample to Robin's inequality exists, then some m>=10080 has f(m)>=e^gamma. Lemma 21: if N<N' are consecutive CA and there is an XA number n>10080 with N<n<N', then N' is XA. Theorem 23: under RH there are infinitely many CA numbers that are also XA.  
  _Proof idea:_ Necessity: if #XA finite then sup f attained, contradicting Gronwall lim sup f = e^gamma. Sufficiency: if RH false, Theorem 6 gives f>=e^gamma at some m and running maxima of f produce an infinite XA sequence only if... — the delicate direction uses Gronwall's theorem and Robin's strict interior bound (Prop 20). Ties infinitude of a specific SA-subsequence to RH.

_Takeaways:_ The cluster is a rigorous, mostly-elementary reduction+computation pipeline that shrinks the search for a Robin counterexample but is structurally incapable of proving RH. Chain of reductions: (a) least counterexample is SA [Akbary-Friggstad, elementary]; (b) it must in fact be a CA number, because between consecutive CA numbers f is dominated by the endpoint CA values [Robin Prop 1 / Nazar Prop 20 strict, elementary convexity]; (c) CA numbers are explicitly enumerable via the epsilon-sweep: F(x,k)=log(1+1/(x+..+x^k))/log x, exponent alpha_p(eps)=floor(log((p^{1+eps}-1)/(p^eps-1))/log p)-1, decreasing eps past each critical value in E adds one prime factor; (d) rigorously evaluate f<e^gamma at each CA number with interval arithmetic, then the gap lemma certifies whole integer intervals. Computational frontier: Briggs 2006 -> n<=10^(10^10); Morrill-Platt 2021 -> n<=10^(10^13.11485) via 100-bit interval arithmetic, equivalently Robin holds for all n<=N_{k0}, k0=999,999,476,056, p_{k0}=29,996,208,012,611 (Axler Lemma 2.3). Parallel unconditional track (t-free ladder): Robin proven for 5/7/11/20/21-free integers, giving RH <=> Robin for all 21-full integers (Axler 2021). The WALL: RH <=> f(n)<e^gamma for ALL n>5040, and Gronwall's unconditional lim sup f = e^gamma means CA numbers sit exactly at the boundary; the sign of the sub-leading term (Ramanujan's S1 = sum over zeta zeros x^{rho-1}/|rho|^2) is what RH governs, so any finite CA verification or elementary asymptotic leaves an infinite tail whose behavior IS the Riemann Hypothesis. Sources fetched and quoted: Axler arXiv:2110.13478v3 (6pp, primary for computational frontier + t-free), Nazardonyavi-Yakubovich arXiv:1211.2147v3 and its JIS Vol.17 Nazar4 companion (CA algorithm eqns (19)-(21), Prop 20 strict gap, XA/RH equivalence).

## Unconditional partial-result families

- **gronwall-maximal-order** *(Gronwall 1913, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 14, 113-122 (restated in Choie-Lichiardopol-Moree-Sole 2007, J. Theor. Nombres Bordeaux 19, 357-372 = MPI-preprint 2006/47, Thm 9(1); Axler 2021 arXiv:2110.13478 eq 1.1))* [elementary]  
  limsup_{n->inf} sigma(n)/(n log log n) = e^gamma, where sigma is the sum-of-divisors function and gamma is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.  
  _Proof idea:_ Evaluate sigma(n)/n on primorials N_k = prod_{p<=x} p; sigma(N_k)/N_k = prod_{p<=x}(1+1/p) ~ prod (1-1/p)^{-1}, then apply Mertens' theorem prod_{p<=x}(1-1/p)^{-1} ~ e^gamma log x and the PNT (theta(x)~x) so log log N_k ~ log x. Gives the limsup; primes give liminf 0.
- **robin-equivalence-RH** *(Robin 1984, J. Math. Pures Appl. (9) 63, 187-213, Theoreme 1 (cited as Prop 1.1 in Axler arXiv:2110.13478; builds on Ramanujan, Ramanujan J. 1 (1997) 119-153))* [deep/analytic]  
  The Riemann Hypothesis is TRUE if and only if Robin's inequality sigma(n) < e^gamma * n * log log n (equivalently sigma(n)/n < e^gamma log log n) holds for every integer n >= 5041. Moreover if RH is false there are infinitely many n violating it.  
  _Proof idea:_ Not elementary. Robin's analysis of the extremal (colossally/super-abundant) numbers and the explicit RH-conditional asymptotic sigma(n)/n <= e^gamma(log log n - 2(sqrt2-1)/sqrt(log n) + ...) due to Ramanujan; the reverse direction (RH false => infinitely many exceptions) uses growth of the error term tied to the real parts of zeta zeros.
- **robin-odd-n>9** *(Choie-Lichiardopol-Moree-Sole 2007, J. Theor. Nombres Bordeaux 19, 357-372 (MPI 2006/47), Theorem 2 (and Theorem 7))* [elementary]  
  Every odd positive integer n distinct from 1, 3, 5 and 9 satisfies Robin's inequality (i.e. every odd n > 9 is in R). It is derived from the stronger Nicolas-type bound: for all odd n except 1,3,5,9,15 one has n/phi(n) < e^gamma log log n.  
  _Proof idea:_ Elementary induction on omega(n) (number of distinct prime factors). Reduce to odd squarefree n=q_1...q_m via the squarefree kernel; use n/phi(n) < (3/2)*prod(q_i+1)/q_i = sigma(n_1)/n_1 with n_1=2n/q_m < n, then invoke the squarefree Theorem 1 and a finite exceptional set S={3^a 5^b q^c}. Uses only Mertens-type Lemma 1 (sum_{p<=x} 1/p <= log log x + gamma for x>=5) plus sigma(n)/n < n/phi(n).
- **robin-squarefree-n>30** *(Choie-Lichiardopol-Moree-Sole 2007, J. Theor. Nombres Bordeaux 19, 357-372 (MPI 2006/47), Theorem 1)* [elementary]  
  Every squarefree integer n not in A = {2,3,5,6,10,30} satisfies Robin's inequality; equivalently every squarefree n > 30 is in R (30 is the largest squarefree exception).  
  _Proof idea:_ Elementary induction on omega(n)=m for squarefree n=q_1...q_m (q_1<...<q_m). Case q_m >= log n: use the induction hypothesis and a convexity/mean-value estimate (log b - log a)/(b-a) > 1/b to bound sigma. Case q_m < log n: reduce to sum_{p<=q_m} 1/p <= gamma + log log q_m < gamma + log log log n via Lemma 1 part 2 (pre-PNT Mertens estimate). Lemma 2 handles r*q with r in A, q>=7 prime.
- **robin-squarefull-exceptions** *(Choie-Lichiardopol-Moree-Sole 2007, J. Theor. Nombres Bordeaux 19, 357-372 (MPI 2006/47), Theorem 4 (and Theorem 8))* [deep/analytic]

Relies on Rosser-Schoenfeld (explicit zero knowledge) so not fully elementary.

- **robin-sum-two-squares-n>720** *(Banks-Hart-Moree-Nevans 2009, Monatsh. Math. 157, 303-322 = arXiv:0710.2424 (MPI 2007/125), Theorem 1 & Theorem 2)* [deep/analytic]

Bound F(n)=n/phi(n) using restricted prime factors with explicit Ramare-Rumely prime-distribution bounds, then finite computation enumerates all exceptions. Uses explicit analytic bounds; elementary in spirit but not pre-PNT.

Not elementary.

## Elementary RH equivalences

- **Gronwall (maximal order of sigma)** *(Gronwall 1913, as stated in Caveney-Nicolas-Sondow, arXiv:1112.6010 (Ramanujan J. 2014), Theorem 1)* [deep/analytic]  
  For n>1, G(n):=sigma(n)/(n log log n) satisfies limsup_{n->infty} G(n) = e^gamma = 1.78107..., where gamma is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.  
  _Proof idea:_ Mertens' theorem for prod_{p<=x}(1-1/p)^{-1} ~ e^gamma log x combined with sigma(n)/n <= prod_{p|n}(1-1/p^{k+1})/(1-1/p); extremal n are primorials. Classical elementary/analytic (PNT-level) argument.
- **Ramanujan (conditional upper order)** *(Ramanujan 1915/1997 (suppressed part of highly composite numbers paper), as stated in Caveney-Nicolas-Sondow, arXiv:1112.6010, Theorem 2)* [deep/analytic]  
  If the Riemann Hypothesis is true, then G(n) < e^gamma for all sufficiently large n (n >> 1).  
  _Proof idea:_ Ramanujan's 1915 study of highly composite numbers gives an asymptotic inequality for sigma on generalized-highly-composite (CA) numbers under RH; uses zero-free region of zeta. Not elementary.
- **Robin's criterion** *(Robin 1984 (J. Math. Pures Appl. 63, 187-213), as stated in Caveney-Nicolas-Sondow arXiv:1112.6010 Theorem 3 and arXiv:1211.6944 (Nicolas-Sondow survey))* [deep/analytic]  
  RH is true if and only if G(n) = sigma(n)/(n log log n) < e^gamma for all n > 5040 (equivalently sigma(n) < e^gamma n log log n for all n>5040). There are exactly 26 known exceptions r with G(r) >= e^gamma (OEIS A067698), all <= 5040.  
  _Proof idea:_ Under RH one sharpens Ramanujan's bound to hold from n>5040; conversely a zero of zeta off the critical line forces infinitely many n>5040 (colossally-abundant-type) violating the bound via an oscillation/Landau argument. Reduces the analytic RH to a single divisor-sum inequality on integers.
- **Robin's unconditional bound** *(Robin 1984, quoted as inequality (2) in Caveney-Nicolas-Sondow arXiv:1112.6010)* [elementary]  
  Unconditionally, for all n>1: G(n) <= e^gamma + 0.6482.../(log log n)^2. Consequently G(n) < e^gamma + 0.6483/(log log 5)^2 = 4.643... for all n>=5.  
  _Proof idea:_ Effective (non-RH) estimate for sigma(n)/n on superabundant numbers using explicit prime-counting bounds. Elementary given effective Mertens/Chebyshev estimates.
- **Nicolas's criterion (totient / primorial form)** *(Nicolas 1983 (Ann. Inst. Fourier 33, and Ramanujan J. work), stated as 'Nicolas's Theorem' in Nicolas-Sondow arXiv:1211.6944)* [deep/analytic]  
  RH is true if and only if for every prime p>2, N_p/(phi(N_p) log log N_p) > e^gamma, where N_p = p# = 2*3*5*...*p is the primorial. Equivalently n/phi(n) < e^gamma log log n fails to be violated beyond the primorials only if RH holds. Also limsup_{n->infty} (n/phi(n))/log log n = e^gamma.  
  _Proof idea:_ Studies the minimal order of phi on primorials; under RH the product prod_{p<=x}(1-1/p)^{-1} stays below the e^gamma log x barrier, while an off-line zero forces infinitely many primorials to exceed it (Landau's Oscillation Theorem, as used for Chebyshev's bias). Robin adapted this to prove his sigma criterion.
- **Nicolas => Robin reduction (sigma/n < n/phi(n))** *(Standard; used by Robin 1984 (advisor Nicolas) per Nicolas-Sondow arXiv:1211.6944)* [elementary]  
  For all n>1, sigma(n)/n < n/phi(n) = prod_{p|n}(1-1/p)^{-1}. Hence on any n, the Nicolas bound n/phi(n) < e^gamma log log n implies Robin's bound sigma(n)/n < e^gamma log log n; on primorials the two quantities have the same e^gamma limiting order, so Nicolas's primorial criterion underlies Robin's.  
  _Proof idea:_ sigma(n)/n = prod_{p^k||n}(1-p^{-(k+1)})/(1-p^{-1}) < prod_{p|n}(1-1/p)^{-1} = n/phi(n) term by term. Purely elementary divisor-product inequality; the primorials are the common extremal family for both.
- **Lagarias's criterion** *(Lagarias 2002 (Amer. Math. Monthly 109, 534-543), arXiv:math/0008177 'An Elementary Problem Equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis')* [elementary]  
  RH is true if and only if sigma(n) <= H_n + exp(H_n) log(H_n) for every integer n>=1, with equality if and only if n=1, where H_n = sum_{k=1}^n 1/k is the n-th harmonic number.  
  _Proof idea:_ Uses H_n = log n + gamma + o(1) so exp(H_n) ~ e^gamma n and H_n + exp(H_n)log H_n ~ e^gamma n log log n; a careful elementary transformation makes the inequality equivalent to Robin's for all n>=1 (folding the small-n exceptions into the sharper harmonic bound).
- **Superabundant (SA) and Colossally Abundant (CA) definitions & structure** *(Alaoglu-Erdos 1944; Ramanujan 1915; restated in Caveney-Nicolas-Sondow arXiv:1112.6010 eqs (4)-(6))* [elementary]  
  N>1 is superabundant (SA) if sigma(N)/N > sigma(n)/n for all 0<n<N. N>1 is colossally abundant (CA) if there exists eps>0 with sigma(N)/N^{1+eps} >= sigma(n)/n^{1+eps} for all n>1. Every CA number is SA. If N = 2^{k2} 3^{k3} ... p^{kp} is SA or CA then k2>=k3>=...>=kp with kp=1 unless N in {4,36}, and the largest prime factor P(N) ~ log N.  
  _Proof idea:_ Alaoglu-Erdos (1944) / Ramanujan: maximizing sigma(n)/n^{1+eps} factorizes independently over primes, forcing non-increasing exponents; CA are the eps-parametrized maximizers hence a subsequence of SA. Elementary optimization over prime exponents.
- **GA1 and GA2 numbers (definitions)** *(Caveney-Nicolas-Sondow arXiv:1112.6010 (Ramanujan J.) and arXiv:1110.5078 (Integers 11 (2011), A33), Definition of extraordinary number)* [elementary]  
  N>1 is a GA1 number if N is composite and G(N) >= G(N/p) for all prime factors p of N. N>1 is a GA2 number if G(N) >= G(aN) for all multiples aN of N. A composite N is 'extraordinary' iff it is both GA1 and GA2, i.e. G(N) >= max(G(N/p), G(aN)) for all prime factors p and all multiples aN. The smallest extraordinary number is 4 (G(4)=5.357...).  
  _Proof idea:_ Definitions isolating local maximality of Gronwall's G downward (divide by a prime) and upward (multiply). 4 is GA1 since G(2)<0<G(4); 4 is GA2 since Robin's unconditional bound gives G(n)<4.643<G(4) for n>=5, and G(3),G(5) are checked directly.
- **CNS extraordinary-number reformulation of RH** *(Caveney-Nicolas-Sondow, arXiv:1110.5078 = Integers 11 (2011) A33, main theorem; restated as Theorem 4 + Corollary 8 in arXiv:1112.6010)* [elementary]  
  (i) RH is true if and only if 4 is the only extraordinary number, i.e. iff 4 is the only composite N satisfying G(N) >= max(G(N/p), G(aN)) for all prime factors p of N and all multiples aN of N. Equivalently, RH iff 4 is the only number that is both GA1 and GA2. (ii) If there is any counterexample to Robin's inequality, then mu := max{G(n): n>5040} exists and the least N>5040 with G(N)=mu is extraordinary.  
  _Proof idea:_ If RH fails, Robin fails, so max mu:=max{G(n):n>5040} is attained (G->e^gamma from Robin's unconditional bound) at some N>5040; that maximizer is >= its divisors N/p and >= all multiples aN, hence extraordinary and !=4, giving a second extraordinary number. Conversely if RH holds, G(n)<e^gamma=G's sup for n>5040 while G(4)>e^gamma, so no N>4 can be simultaneously GA1 and GA2. Uses Robin + Gronwall; alternate proof of one step uses SA-number properties (Alaoglu-Erdos).
- **GA2 numbers characterize RH failure quantitatively** *(Caveney-Nicolas-Sondow arXiv:1112.6010, abstract + Theorem 5 (OEIS A197369))* [elementary]  
  There are exactly nineteen known GA2 numbers <= 5040: N = 3,4,5,6,8,10,12,18,24,36,48,60,72,120,180,240,360,2520,5040; every GA2 number > 5 is even. A GA2 number N > 5040 exists if and only if RH is false, in which case N is even and N > 10^8576.  
  _Proof idea:_ A GA2 number >5040 is a local upward-maximum of G exceeding the n=5040 region, which can only occur if Robin's inequality is violated (RH false); parity from sigma(2N)=3sigma(N) for odd N; the 10^8576 lower bound comes from the numerical range (up to e^19747) over which Robin's inequality is verified true.
- **Infinitely many CA numbers are GA1** *(Caveney-Nicolas-Sondow arXiv:1112.6010, Theorem 6)* [elementary]  
  Infinitely many colossally abundant numbers are GA1. (The smallest GA1 number with more than two prime factors is 183783600; the smallest odd GA1 number is 1058462574572984015114271643676625.)  
  _Proof idea:_ For a CA number N of parameter eps with largest prime p, log(N/p) is large enough that G(N/q) < G(N) for every prime factor q, using the CA exponent structure and effective estimates on the CA parameter function F(p,1). Elementary given the SA/CA machinery.

_Takeaways:_ This cluster is a web of EXACT RH-equivalences all pivoting on Gronwall's function G(n)=sigma(n)/(n loglog n) and its extremal sequence of colossally abundant numbers. Logical spine: Nicolas (1983, totient/primorials) => Robin (1984, sigma/n) is elementary via sigma(n)/n < n/phi(n) with a shared e^gamma extremal order; Robin is the hub. Lagarias (2002) is Robin re-dressed in harmonic numbers (elementary equivalence). Caveney-Nicolas-Sondow (2011 Integers A33 / 2012 Ramanujan J.) give the newest reformulation: RH <=> 4 is the only 'extraordinary' number (= only composite that is both GA1 [G(N)>=G(N/p)] and GA2 [G(N)>=G(aN)]); its easy direction is genuinely elementary, its content inherited from Robin+Gronwall. Quantitatively, only 26 known n exceed e^gamma and a Robin/GA2 counterexample must exceed 10^8576. HONEST BOTTOM LINE: every member is a biconditional; proving RH through them means proving an inequality for ALL n, and the sole obstruction sits exactly on the CA subsequence where an off-line zero would inject a Landau oscillation above e^gamma — so 'CA asymptotics of G below e^gamma' IS RH. The elementarity is in the reformulation, never in a path to the proof.

## THE WALL (why none of this reaches RH)

**Unconditional partial-result families:** Why none of these reach RH (and precisely why CA/superabundant asymptotics = RH):

1. STRUCTURE OF THE WALL. Robin's equivalence (robin-equivalence-RH) says RH <=> Robin's inequality for ALL n>=5041. Each partial result carves out a family F and proves the inequality on F unconditionally. But the smallest hypothetical counterexample is forced (Alaoglu-Erdos / Robin) to be a SUPERABUNDANT (indeed essentially colossally abundant) number: an integer with e1>=e2>=...>=es (Hardy-Ramanujan exponent pattern), largest-prime exponent 1, and rapidly growing small-prime exponents. Every family here is precisely COMPLEMENTARY to that danger zone: 'odd', 'squarefree', 't-free', 'sum of two squares', 'bounded vp' all EXCLUDE the highly-composite/highly-abundant numbers where the extremal ratio lives. The uncovered set is the t-FULL / high-valuation / super-abundant tail, which is exactly where RH's truth is encoded.

2. t-FREE CAN NEVER CLOSE IT UNAIDED. For each fixed t, Axler's Remark (from Lemma 2.4) proves lim_{k->inf} R_t(N_k) = e^gamma/zeta(t) < e^gamma. So t-free is provable for EVERY fixed t by pure computation — but the extremal (colossally abundant) numbers have unbounded maximal exponent max(e_i) -> infinity along any sequence of Robin exceptions (CLMS Remark after Lemma 6: if RH false, the exceptions n=prod p_i^{e_i} satisfy max e_i -> infinity). No finite t captures numbers of unbounded exponent, so the t-free ladder (5,7,11,20,21-free,...) approaches but never covers the full set. Same for the vp-valuation bounds (Hertlein/Axler Thm 1.4): each caps finitely many primes' exponents; the RH-critical numbers need arbitrarily large exponents on arbitrarily many small primes.

3. WHY 'CA ASYMPTOTICS = RH'. On colossally abundant numbers the quantity to control is sigma(N)/N - e^gamma log log N. Ramanujan's RH-conditional expansion gives sigma(n)/n <= e^gamma(log log n - 2(sqrt2-1)/sqrt(log n) + S1(log n)/... ) where S1(x)=sum_rho x^{rho-1}/|rho|^2 sums over the NON-TRIVIAL ZETA ZEROS rho. If RH holds (Re rho = 1/2) this error term is O(1/sqrt(log n)) and negative-dominated => inequality holds for all large CA numbers. If some zero has Re rho = 1/2+delta>0, the corresponding term x^{rho-1} = x^{-1/2+delta} decays too slowly and the sign/size of the oscillation eventually pushes sigma(n)/n ABOVE e^gamma log log n on infinitely many CA numbers. So the inequality on the CA/superabundant tail is LITERALLY controlled by the real parts of the zeta zeros — that is why proving it there is exactly RH, and why every family that avoids that tail gives '<half a proof' (CLMS: even/non-squarefree density 0.2973...; Thm 3 reduces RH to even non-squarefree n>=5044). 

4. The reductions (CLMS Thm 6: RH <=> Robin for all 5th-power-divisible n; Axler Cor 2.5: RH <=> Robin for all 21-full n) make the wall explicit: they PROVE that the ENTIRE difficulty has been relocated into the t-full complement, not diminished. Briggs/Morrill-Platt only verify a FINITE initial range of CA numbers (up to ~N_{999,999,476,056}); RH concerns the infinite tail beyond any computed bound.

**Elementary RH equivalences:** Why none of these reach RH (they are equivalences, not proofs): Each result replaces the analytic statement 'all nontrivial zeros of zeta have Re s = 1/2' with an arithmetic inequality on sigma/phi, but the reduction is a BICONDITIONAL whose 'hard' direction (RH => inequality) still rests on Ramanujan/Robin's conditional analysis of Gronwall's G along colossally-abundant numbers, and whose converse (inequality => RH) rests on Landau's Oscillation Theorem. So proving RH via this cluster requires proving the inequality for ALL n, which is exactly as hard as RH.\n\nWhy 'CA asymptotics = RH' precisely (the robin-gronwall wall): The colossally abundant numbers are the extremal sequence for G(n)=sigma(n)/(n loglog n) (they maximize sigma(n)/n^{1+eps}), so sup_n G(n) is realized in the limit along CA numbers and equals e^gamma (Gronwall). Robin's criterion says RH <=> G(n)<e^gamma for all n>5040; the ONLY place this can fail is along the CA/superabundant subsequence (elsewhere G is bounded well below e^gamma by the unconditional bound e^gamma+0.6482/(loglog n)^2). The precise link: log(sigma(n)/n) on CA numbers = sum over primes of an explicit term, and controlling its deviation from e^gamma loglog n is equivalent, via an explicit-formula / Mellin transform of the prime sum, to bounding sum over zeta-zeros of x^rho/rho, i.e. to the location of the zeros. An off-critical-line zero rho=beta+i*gamma with beta>1/2 produces an oscillation of amplitude ~ x^{beta-1/2}/... in the prime sum (Landau's theorem), which forces infinitely many CA numbers to violate G<e^gamma. Hence 'the CA-number asymptotics of G stay below e^gamma' and 'RH' are literally the same statement — the CA sequence is where the RH oscillation term is exposed. Robin's inequality does not tame that oscillation; it only re-encodes it.\n\nAdditional walls: (1) The extraordinary/GA1/GA2 reformulation (CNS) is elegant and its EASY direction (RH => 4 unique) is a short elementary argument, but the substantive content is entirely inherited from Robin (used as a black box), so it adds no new leverage on the zeros. (2) The '19 GA2 numbers, next one >10^8576 iff RH false' result shows any counterexample is astronomically large (consistent with numerical verification of Robin up to ~e^19747), but a lower bound on a hypothetical counterexample is not a proof no counterexample exists. (3) Lagarias's harmonic-number form is cosmetically elementary (only H_n, exp, log, sigma) but is provably EQUIVALENT to Robin by an elementary transformation — it cannot be easier than RH. (4) Nicolas's totient criterion (on primorials) and the elementary inequality sigma(n)/n < n/phi(n) give Nicolas => Robin cheaply on the extremal family, but the converse/hard direction again invokes Landau oscillation, so it too is pinned to the zeros.

**Robin/Gronwall + the wall:** WHY CA ASYMPTOTICS = RH (the wall). Broughan Thm 1 (least counterexample can be taken CA) + Akbary-Friggstad Thm 3 (least counterexample is superabundant) reduce RH to a statement purely about the sparse CA sequence: RH TRUE <=> G(n)<e^gamma for ALL CA n>5040. Robin's own conditional dichotomy is the wall itself: if RH is FALSE then (Robin Thm 7) G(n) exceeds the HIGHER threshold e^gamma(1+c/(log n)^b) for INFINITELY MANY n (b in (1-theta,1/2), theta=sup Re(zero)>1/2); if RH is TRUE then G(n)<e^gamma for all CA n>5040. So controlling limsup of G on CA numbers as n->infinity is logically equivalent to RH, and the gap between the two regimes is governed by theta i.e. by the width of the zeta zero-free region. The e^gamma limsup (Gronwall) is unconditional; the SHARP e^gamma at finite CA numbers is exactly the analytic content of RH.\n\nWHY ZIMOV'S THEOREM 8 DOES NOT PROVE RH. This is the critical honesty point. Theorem 8 does NOT rule out CA counterexamples; it only proves that IF a least CA counterexample n exists, it must lie in the BAND e^gamma < G(n) < e^gamma(1+c/(log n)^b) and therefore CANNOT simultaneously be a member of Robin's Theorem-7 infinite set that sits ABOVE the higher threshold. Logically this is a consistency/separation result: the local step-ratio bound G(n)/G(m)=1+O(1/log n) (from p~log n and consecutive-CA structure) is INCOMPATIBLE with an O(1/(log n)^b), b<1/2, jump above threshold in a single CA step. But (i) the band [e^gamma, e^gamma(1+c/(log n)^b)) is NON-EMPTY, so a counterexample with G(n) just barely >= e^gamma is NOT excluded; (ii) Theorem 7's 'infinitely many n exceeding the higher threshold' need not include the FIRST/LEAST CA violation - a single first violation can sit just above e^gamma while the guaranteed infinite family of large excesses occurs later, so no contradiction with RH-false arises. Hence Theorem 8 leaves the RH-false world logically consistent: it constrains the least CA counterexample's location but does not force its non-existence. The paper explicitly frames Thm 8 as a 'constraint' / 'exclusion from the infinite set beyond the higher threshold', NOT as a proof that no counterexample exists. Broughan's contrapositive (no CA counterexample => RH) is what WOULD close it, and Theorem 8 does not establish that antecedent.\n\nADDITIONAL CAVEAT: 'least CA counterexample' is not necessarily the least counterexample overall (could be superabundant-but-not-CA); Zimov notes this in the Remark after Def 2.2. The finite verification (Morrill-Platt, up to 10^(10^13.099)) is only a lower bound on where any counterexample could start, not a proof of none.

**SA/CA reduction, gap lemma, computations:** Why none of this reaches RH (robin-gronwall-wall). (1) Robin's criterion is a REFORMULATION, not a weakening: RH <=> f(n)<e^gamma for all n>5040. Proving RH via this route requires establishing the inequality for ALL n>5040, i.e. an infinite verification. Every computational result (Briggs 10^(10^10), Morrill-Platt 10^(10^13.11485)) only certifies a finite initial segment. The gap lemma reduces 'all integers' to 'all CA numbers', but there are still infinitely many CA numbers, so the frontier is always finite and RH-agnostic. (2) The Gronwall wall: lim sup f = e^gamma is UNCONDITIONAL and the lim sup is approached exactly along CA/superabundant numbers. So CA numbers are precisely where f is largest and comes arbitrarily close to e^gamma from BELOW under RH — but Gronwall alone cannot decide whether f ever reaches or exceeds e^gamma for large n. The margin f(N_k) - e^gamma has the same sign structure as the RH excess term; controlling it for ALL large primorials is equivalent to bounding sums over zeta zeros (Ramanujan's conditional expansion sigma(n)/n <= e^gamma(log log n - 2(sqrt2-1)/sqrt(log n) + S1(log n)+...) with S1 = sum over nontrivial zeros rho of x^{rho-1}/|rho|^2). Thus 'CA asymptotics decide RH' precisely because the sign of the sub-leading term is governed by the real parts of zeta zeros — an unconditional CA asymptotic that pins the sign for all k would BE a proof of RH, and no such asymptotic is known. (3) The t-free ladder (5,7,11,20,21-free; Axler Cor 2.5: RH <=> Robin for all 21-full integers) narrows the residual set but the residual (t-full / 21-full integers for all t) is still infinite; each increment of t needs stronger effective ϑ/prime-product estimates and never closes because e^gamma/zeta(t) -> e^gamma as t->inf, so the safety margin R_t(N_k)-e^gamma shrinks to 0 and the required k0(t) -> infinity. (4) The XA equivalence (RH <=> #XA=infinity) is again a translation of RH into an infinitude statement about a specific SA-subsequence; it is 'fragile' (the paper's own Thms 33/36 show nearby SA-subsequences are unconditionally infinite), i.e. it does not isolate a decidable finite object. (5) Every genuinely new theorem here is elementary/effective-analytic (Chebyshev ϑ bounds, Mertens, Euler product) and deliberately AVOIDS the zero-free region; that is exactly why they cannot cross the wall — RH is precisely the input they refuse to assume, and the unconditional side of each result stops short at O(1/(log log n)^k) error terms whose sign is what RH controls.


## What this does NOT prove

- Nothing here proves RH; `conjecture1_proved` = False.
- Computational verifications (Briggs; Morrill-Platt) are FLOATING-POINT / interval-arithmetic over a FINITE range -- empirical, not kernel-checked theorems.
- Partial-result families are RESTRICTED-SCOPE (odd, squarefree, t-free, p-adic-bounded); their union deliberately EXCLUDES the CA/SA hard core, so they cannot cover all n.
- The equivalences (Lagarias, Nicolas, CNS) relocate RH's difficulty into arithmetic; the hard direction still rests on the CA asymptotics (Ramanujan/Robin) and the converse on Landau's oscillation theorem.
- A finite verified range is CONSISTENT with RH; it is not evidence FOR RH, because the difficult (large-CA) cases are never sampled by any finite prefix.
