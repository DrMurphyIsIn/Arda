\begin{abstract}
We present \Telperion, a tool that turns families of concrete mathematical
statements into machine-checked \Lean{} theorems by \emph{exact-arithmetic
certificate and kernel verification}. The design separates a large, replaceable
\emph{generator} from a small \emph{trusted core}: the generator searches for and
serializes a certificate (a sum-of-squares decomposition, a Positivstellensatz
or Farkas combination, a Wilf--Zeilberger pair, a Chv\'atal--Gomory derivation,
and roughly thirty other shapes), and \Lean's kernel re-derives the theorem from
that certificate. A defective certificate is therefore a \emph{compile failure},
never a false theorem; the generator is untrusted by construction, and the same
pipeline proves an unrelated textbook inequality and a research-scale result
through identical machinery.

Beyond kernel acceptance, \Telperion's output can be handed to an
\emph{independent judge}: OpenAI's \Comparator{} exports each proof and checks
that it proves exactly the intended statement, uses only a whitelist of axioms,
and survives replay by a second kernel (\nanoda, an independent Rust
reimplementation) in addition to \Lean's own. A soundness bug would have to fool
two independently written kernels.

As a flagship demonstration we report the state of a formalization of the
Brualdi--Goldwasser extremal-tree conjecture. We are explicit about what this
demonstrates: the conjecture is \emph{not} proved. What is proved --- a
conditional reduction of the full conjecture to two precisely stated open layers,
together with the two analytic cruxes it rests on --- is independently verified by
both kernels and is axiom-clean. The contribution we emphasize is exactly this:
a methodology in which the boundary between what is proved and what is open is
enforced by the kernel and by an independent second kernel, and is therefore
impossible to overstate.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Disclosure.} \Telperion{} and the accompanying formalization

incidental to the paper's thesis: the trust model is designed precisely so that
the generator --- whether a human, a solver, or an AI --- is untrusted, and
soundness rests only on \Lean's kernel and an independent second kernel. See
