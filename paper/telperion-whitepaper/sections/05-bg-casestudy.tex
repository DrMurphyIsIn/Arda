\section{Flagship case study: the Brualdi--Goldwasser Laplacian-ratio problem}
\label{sec:bg}

We use an in-progress attack on the Brualdi--Goldwasser tree-maximization problem
as the paper's research-scale demonstration. The area is live and moving fast---a
2025 conjecture about the maximizer was refuted in 2026---which makes it an
unusually good test of the methodology's central promise: keeping a machine-checked
boundary between what is proved and what is merely believed. We state plainly what
is and is not established. \textbf{The maximizer is not determined here}; what is
established is a new structural narrowing of the problem (verified in exact
arithmetic) and a conditional formalization of a candidate's global optimality
(verified by two kernels), with the gap between them explicit.

\subsection{The problem and its recent history}

Brualdi and Goldwasser~\cite{brualdigoldwasser} introduced the study of the
permanent of the Laplacian of a tree and, among other results, asked for the tree
on $n$ vertices maximizing the \emph{Laplacian ratio}
\[
  \pi(T) \;=\; \frac{\operatorname{per}(L(T))}{\prod_{v} \deg(v)},
  \qquad L = D - A .
\]
The maximizer is open. Wu, Dong and Lai~\cite{wudonglai} conjectured it to be a
subdivided star. Pant~\cite{pant} refuted that conjecture in 2026 with families of
\emph{spider} trees $T(a_1,\dots,a_m)$ (a path spine whose vertices carry length-two
cherries) that beat the conjectured subdivided star; Pant proposes no replacement,
and the true maximizer remains open. This is the state of the art the formalization
engages.

\subsection{An exact-arithmetic narrowing: branching beats spiders}

The project's first contribution is computational but \emph{exact}---rational
arithmetic throughout, no floating point, so ``beats'' is a strict rational
inequality with no margin. It shows the maximizer is not a path-spine spider at all.
Concretely, for a branching family $B(3,t)$---a degree-three hub whose four centers
each carry $t$ cherries, on $n = 8t+4$ vertices---one has, for \emph{every} integer
$t \ge 3$,
\[
  \pi\bigl(B(3,t)\bigr) - \pi\bigl(T(t,t,t,t)\bigr)
  \;=\; \Bigl(\tfrac32\Bigr)^{4t} \cdot
        \frac{2\,P(t)}{81\,(t+1)^3 (t+2)^2 (t+3)},
  \qquad P(t) = 56t^4 + 72t^3 - 162t^2 - 432t - 243,
\]
and $P(t) > 0$ for all $t \ge 3$ (the prefactor and denominator are positive, and
$P$ is increasing on $[3,\infty)$ with $P(3) > 0$). Since $T(t,t,t,t)$ is exactly
Pant's flagship counterexample family, this proves \emph{no member of that family is
the maximizer}: the maximizer is a branching tree, not a path-spine spider. This is
a genuine advance on the published state, and it is the kind of exact-certificate
computation \Telperion{} is built for. Two independent permanent engines (a tree
matching-sum recursion and Ryser's formula on the full Laplacian) agree on the
values, an exhaustive search confirms the true maximizer for small $n$ (where it
\emph{is} a spider, exhibiting a genuine path-to-branch transition as $n$ grows), and
the pipeline reproduces Pant's published rational values exactly.

We are careful about the verification \emph{tier} here: this narrowing is an
exact-rational computation, machine-checked in the project's certified arithmetic,
but it is a computation, not (yet) a \Lean{} kernel proof. It motivates a candidate
maximizer---a branching-backbone family---whose \emph{global} optimality is the
subject of the formalization below.

\subsection{The conditional formalization and its two open layers}

Establishing that the candidate is the global maximizer---$\forall t,\ \mathrm{Aobj}(t)
\le \mathrm{Aobj}(\mathrm{tieU})$ for the candidate's value $\mathrm{tieU}$---is the
hard, open part. The formalization's spine is a single \Lean{} theorem,
\code{R3Cert.Step3.conjecture1\_of\_layers}, which is \emph{conditional}: it derives
global optimality from two hypotheses, and is itself sorry-free. Verbatim, it takes a
tie witness \code{tieU} and
\begin{lstlisting}
Hnorm : forall t : UTree, exists s : List Hub,
          Balanced s /\ Capped s /\ Aobj t <= Aobj (backboneU s)
Hdom  : forall s : List Hub, Balanced s -> Capped s ->
          (forall u, not (OrderedStep s u)) -> Aobj (backboneU s) <= Aobj tieU
\end{lstlisting}
and concludes \code{forall t : UTree, Aobj t <= Aobj tieU}. The two hypotheses are the
open layers: $H_{\mathrm{norm}}$ (every tree is dominated by a balanced, capped
canonical hub-state) and $H_{\mathrm{dom}}$ (every merge-normal such state is
dominated by the tie value). Both remain open; a single-hub within-one balancing
argument inside $H_{\mathrm{norm}}$ is formalized and machine-checked, but the pieces
that would close the layer---pinning the extremal arm rates to the specific
$\{4,5\}$ profile, the capping invariant, the tree-to-hub reduction, and multi-hub
extremality---are research-open. The end-goal predicate \code{conjecture1\_proved} is
defined to be \code{False} and asserted so by a test.

\Telperion{} supplies the concrete certificate-bearing pieces of the reduction: the
exact-rational positivity floors of the per-class ledger, the per-rule reduction
certificates of the internal argument, and the two analytic inequalities the
reduction rests on, each an instance of the shapes of \S\ref{sec:emitters}. Two of
those inequalities are the anchor theorems: \code{R3Cert.phi\_le\_one}, the bound
$\log\Phi(b) \le 0$ (i.e.\ $\Phi \le 1$) for every branch, and
\code{R3Cert.CappedJointConfig.gstep\_le\_one\_achievable}, the g-step / master
inequality for achievable configurations.

\paragraph{The model-to-object caveat.} The analytic bounds are proved about an
exact-rational cavity/matching \emph{model} (written $\mathrm{Aobj}$). Identifying
this model with $\pi(T)$ itself is a separate step that is not yet formalized in
\Lean, and we flag it rather than eliding it. The model's numerics are cross-checked
against the exact integer Laplacian permanent by the two independent engines above,
which is evidence---but not a formal proof---of the identification.

\subsection{Machine-verification evidence}
\label{sec:bg-evidence}

The three \Lean{} anchors are the theorems the formalization's continuous integration
guards, and they are re-verified through the \Comparator{} (\S\ref{sec:indep}) on
every change. For each, \code{lean4export} exports the proof, the axiom set is checked
against the clean whitelist, and the exported term is replayed by both \Lean's kernel
and \nanoda. In our runs all three pass: both kernels accept each, and each reports the
axiom trace $[\code{propext}, \code{Quot.sound}, \code{Classical.choice}]$---no
\code{sorryAx}, no \code{native\_decide}. The conditional capstone's dependency cone is
large but tractable: export and two-kernel replay complete in a few minutes of
continuous-integration time. Because these statements are phrased in the
formalization's own vocabulary, the \Comparator{} runs them in self-check mode
(\S\ref{sec:indep}): the axiom whitelist and the independent second kernel are real
verification; independent statement authorship is not available for a statement about
the project's own structures, and we do not claim it.

The resulting claim is precise and unfakeable. We do not assert the global maximizer.
We assert: (i) in exact arithmetic, a branching tree beats the published spider
counterexamples, narrowing the open problem; and (ii) in \Lean, verified by two
independent kernels with a clean axiom trace, a conditional reduction of the
candidate's global optimality to two stated layers, together with the two analytic
cruxes it rests on. Everything else---the two layers, the model-to-object
identification, and hence global optimality itself---is open, and the boundary is
enforced mechanically rather than asserted in prose. A separate paper will report a
theorem on the maximizer only if and when that boundary moves.
