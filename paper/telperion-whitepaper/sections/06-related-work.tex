\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Interactive theorem proving and its libraries.} \Telperion{} targets
\Lean~4~\cite{lean4} and \Mathlib~\cite{mathlib}; its trust argument is the
long-standing de~Bruijn criterion~\cite{debruijn}---a small trusted kernel
re-checks the output of untrusted producers. What \Telperion{} adds at this level
is not a new foundation but a disciplined \emph{producer}: a certificate compiler
whose emitted proofs use only a narrow, fixed repertoire of kernel-backed tactics,
so that the object the kernel checks is always a certificate re-derivation rather
than an opaque tactic script.

\paragraph{Learned provers.} Recent systems---AlphaProof~\cite{alphaproof},
DeepSeek-Prover~\cite{deepseekprover}, and related LLM-driven approaches---\emph{search}
for proofs, typically as tactic sequences, guided by learned models. \Telperion{}
occupies a different point in the design space: it does not search proof \emph{text};
it computes a \emph{certificate} (often via an untrusted numeric solver) and emits a
fixed proof skeleton that re-checks it. The two are complementary---a learned system
could propose the family or the certificate shape, and \Telperion{} could compile
and independently verify the result---and the independent-verification layer of
\S\ref{sec:indep} is exactly the kind of external check that machine-proposed proofs
most need.

\paragraph{Tactic-level automation and Positivstellensatz methods.} \Lean{} and its
neighbors provide \code{positivity}, \code{polyrith}, \code{nlinarith}, and related
tactics; the formal-methods literature includes substantial work on
sum-of-squares and Positivstellensatz certificates and their verified
checking~\cite{positivstellensatz}. \Telperion's contribution relative to these is
integrative rather than a single new procedure: it packages exact certificate
\emph{search-and-rounding} for roughly thirty shapes behind one enforced pipeline,
with provenance, non-vacuity gates, and independent re-verification.

\paragraph{Certificate checking in optimization.} The separation of an untrusted
solver from a trusted, exact checker is standard in optimization---VIPR~\cite{vipr}
provides checkable certificates for mixed-integer programming, and \Telperion's
Chv\'atal--Gomory emitter is VIPR-flavored. \Telperion{} carries that discipline
into general theorem proving: the numeric solver is a search oracle, and only an
exact, kernel-re-checkable certificate crosses into the trusted region.

\paragraph{Independent proof judging.} The independent-verification layer builds
directly on the \Comparator~\cite{comparator}, \code{lean4export}~\cite{lean4export},
and \nanoda~\cite{nanoda}. Our contribution there is the \emph{bridge}---turning
\Telperion's emit results into \Comparator{} challenges automatically---and the
demonstration that a research-scale proof's anchors survive a second, independent
kernel with a clean axiom trace.
