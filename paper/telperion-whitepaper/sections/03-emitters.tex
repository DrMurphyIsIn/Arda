\section{Certificate families and emitters}
\label{sec:emitters}

\Telperion{} turns a problem into \Lean{} when it can be expressed as a
\emph{certifiable family}---a grid of instances, each reducible to one of a fixed
set of certificate \emph{shapes}. This section describes the pipeline, the shapes,
and a fully worked example.

\subsection{The pipeline}

Every family flows through an enforced, four-stage workflow:
\[
\texttt{define} \;\to\; \texttt{certify()} \;\to\; \texttt{validate} \;\to\;
\texttt{emit()} \;\to\; \texttt{lake build} \;\to\; \texttt{freeze()}.
\]
\texttt{define} states the family: its symbols, the grid of parameter values, and
a target expression per grid point. \texttt{certify()} searches for and checks a
certificate for each instance in exact arithmetic, returning a certified-family
witness or naming the first cell (and, where relevant, the corner) that failed.
\texttt{validate} runs the family's own exact-numeric assertions. \texttt{emit()}
renders the certified family into \Lean{} through the emitters; it refuses without
both the certified-family witness and a green validation report, and it applies the
non-vacuity and soundness gates of \S\ref{sec:trust}. \texttt{lake build}---the
user's own continuous integration---is the trusted step: \Mathlib's kernel
re-derives every emitted theorem. \texttt{freeze()} records the output and its
provenance hash for drift detection.

The proof text each emitter writes is deliberately narrow and uniform: identities
are discharged by \code{simp only $\to$ push\_cast $\to$ field\_simp $\to$ ring},
nonnegativity by \code{positivity}, finite case analysis by \code{interval\_cases},
and so on. The generator does not emit clever proofs; it emits certificates and lets
a small, fixed repertoire of kernel-backed tactics re-check them.

\subsection{The certificate shapes}

The shapes fall into a few broad families; Appendix~\ref{app:emitters} gives the
full catalog. Positivity and inequalities are covered by rational-function
positivity via P\'olya lifting (multiply through by $(1+\sum x_i)^N$ and reduce to
a nonnegative-coefficient form), exact rational SOS (which reaches interior
equality cases that P\'olya lifting cannot), Artin-denominator rational SOS for
polynomials that are nonnegative but not SOS (\Telperion{} finds a positive
multiplier $q$ with $q\cdot p$ a sum of squares), Putinar and Handelman
certificates on semialgebraic and polytope constraints, Bernstein positivity on an
interval, and Sturm-sequence root exclusion for strict positivity. Exact algebra is
covered by identity and $p$-adic-valuation emitters, Farkas / linear-Positivstellensatz
cone combinations, Nullstellensatz and real-Nullstellensatz ideal-membership
certificates (cofactors computed by Gr\"obner reduction), and SOS-refutation
certificates of semialgebraic infeasibility. Combinatorial and integer shapes
include Wilf--Zeilberger hypergeometric-sum identities, Chv\'atal--Gomory integer
rounding (VIPR-style), Newton/interlacing inequalities, tangent-line convex-sum
inequalities, Cauchy--Schwarz, and explicit PSD quadratic forms. Finally, assembly
shapes---finite case dispatch, region subdivision and gluing, $\forall K \ge K_0$
tails, substitution glue, and custom skeletons---compose the atomic shapes into
statements in the original variables.

Where a numeric solver is involved (finding a Putinar multiplier by SDP, say), the
solver runs untrusted: its floating-point solution is rounded to exact rationals
and \emph{re-verified exactly} before emission, and only the exact certificate
reaches \Lean. The SDP is a search oracle, not part of the trusted chain.

\subsection{A worked example}
\label{sec:worked}

Take Bernoulli's inequality in integer form: for every integer $k \ge 2$ and real
$x \ge 0$,
\[
(1+x)^k - 1 - kx \;\ge\; 0.
\]
Expanding, $(1+x)^k = \sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}x^j$; the degree-$0$ and degree-$1$
terms cancel against $-1-kx$, leaving
\[
(1+x)^k - 1 - kx \;=\; \sum_{j=2}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\,x^j,
\]
a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients over $x \ge 0$. That is exactly
a P\'olya certificate: the emitter rewrites the target into its
nonnegative-coefficient form and closes each instance by \code{positivity}. Driving
the grid $k \in \{2,\dots,6\}$ through the pipeline yields five \Lean{} theorems of
the form
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem bernoulli_k3 (x : R) (hx : 0 <= x) :
    0 <= 3 * x ^ 2 + x ^ 3 := by
  ...  -- rewrite to the certified form, then `positivity`
\end{lstlisting}
The example is deliberately humble, but it exercises the \emph{general} core (not
any problem-specific machinery), and it makes a soundness point concrete: the
$k=1$ instance is the vacuous $0 \le 0$, and \Telperion{} excludes it---Bernoulli's
inequality is nontrivial only for $k \ge 2$. Harder shapes behave identically from
the caller's view: the Motzkin polynomial $x^4y^2 + x^2y^4 - 3x^2y^2 + 1$, the
canonical nonnegative-but-not-SOS example, is handled by the Artin-denominator
rational-SOS emitter, which finds a positive multiplier turning it into a sum of
squares; the caller sees the same \code{certify $\to$ emit} interface and a
kernel-checked theorem at the end.
