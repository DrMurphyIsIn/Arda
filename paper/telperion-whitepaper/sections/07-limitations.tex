\section{Limitations and scope}
\label{sec:limits}

We are deliberate about what \Telperion{} is not, because a tool whose purpose is
trustworthiness must be honest about its own boundaries.

\paragraph{It proves certifiable families, not arbitrary theorems.} \Telperion{}
applies when a statement can be cast as a grid of instances, each reducible to one
of its certificate shapes---concrete inequalities, identities, valuation facts,
finite case analyses, and their assemblies. It is not a general-purpose prover and
does not attempt open-ended proof search; a statement outside the shape library
requires a new emitter (which the trust model then covers for free) or does not fit.

\paragraph{Vacuity is guarded, not eliminated.} The kernel cannot detect a
true-but-vacuous statement, and \Telperion's non-vacuity checks catch a specific
syntactic class (reflexive statements, non-load-bearing certificates). An
independently authored challenge module (\S\ref{sec:indep}) strengthens this to a
generator-independent statement-identity check---but only when the statement can be
re-stated without reusing the generator's own definitions. Where it cannot (the
capstone of \S\ref{sec:bg}), self-check mode gives the axiom whitelist and the second
kernel but not independent statement authorship, and we say so.

\paragraph{The independent check has its own trusted surface.} The second-kernel
guarantee rests on \code{lean4export}, the \Comparator, and \nanoda. These are
independent of \Telperion's generator---which is the point; a common-mode bug in
\Lean's own kernel would have to be shared by an unrelated Rust implementation to
pass---but they are not themselves free of trust. What the layer buys is the
reduction of a single point of failure to the intersection of two independent ones,
not its elimination.

\paragraph{Scale.} Exporting and re-checking a large proof's dependency cone is not
free: the Brualdi--Goldwasser capstone exports to a substantial term file and takes
minutes of the second kernel's time. This is affordable as a continuous-integration
gate on a set of anchor theorems, but exporting every lemma of a large library on
every commit would not be, and we do not.

\paragraph{The case study is incomplete.} As \S\ref{sec:bg} states, the
Laplacian-ratio maximizer is not determined; two layers ($H_{\mathrm{norm}}$,
$H_{\mathrm{dom}}$) and a model-to-object identification remain open. What is
established---an exact-rational narrowing of the problem, and a two-kernel-verified
conditional reduction---demonstrates the methodology and the auditability of a
partial result, not a resolution of the problem.
