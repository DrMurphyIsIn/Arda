\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Telperion is a small idea taken seriously: if a generator hands you a certificate
instead of an argument, you can accept its work without trusting it, because a
trusted kernel re-checks the certificate from scratch. Built into a tool, that
idea scales to hundreds of kernel-checked inequalities across quite different kinds
of mathematics---combinatorial, analytic, and proof-complexity---with a
self-verification layer for the one thing a kernel cannot see, and an explicit
account of the one thing no machinery can settle.

The three case studies are, we hope, honest advertisements for both the tool and
the posture. The Brualdi--Goldwasser analytic core is machine-checked and
tie-tight, and the classical conjecture is open, with the missing bridge named.
The zeta zero-free regions are unconditional and kernel-checked, and weaker than
the classical result, and not a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis. The
proof-complexity lower bound is verified symbolically in $n$, and the mathematics
is someone else's. In each case the interesting content and the honest boundary
are stated in the same breath---which is exactly what a certificate engine, whose
value is that you need not trust it, ought to make easy. The open fronts stay open;
we would rather leave them clearly marked than quietly filled.
