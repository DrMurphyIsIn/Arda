\section{Case study I: Brualdi--Goldwasser}
\label{sec:bg}

Our first case study is the problem that started the campaign, and it is also the
one where being scrupulous about \emph{what is and isn't proved} matters most.
Let us be clear at the outset: the classical conjecture is \textbf{open}. What we
report is a body of machine-checked analytic content and an honest account of the
gap that remains.

\subsection{The question}

In 1984, Brualdi and Goldwasser asked which tree maximizes a particular ratio
\cite{brualdi1984}. For a tree $T$ on $n$ vertices, write $L(T)$ for its
Laplacian and consider
\[
  \pi(T) \;=\; \frac{\operatorname{per} L(T)}{\prod_{v} \deg(v)},
\]
the \emph{permanent} of the Laplacian normalized by the degree product. Which
tree makes it largest?

Why is this hard? Because the determinant of the same matrix is useless here: for
a random-walk normalization $\det(I - P) = 0$ for every graph, so every tool that
reasons through determinants---eigenvalues, the matrix-tree theorem, the whole
spectral toolbox---sees nothing at all. The entire content lives in the sign
cancellations the determinant throws away and the permanent keeps. Permanents do
not factor and do not telescope, and this problem sits exactly where that
stubbornness bites.

\subsection{What the answer looks like}

The conjectured maximizer is a \emph{near-star}: a hub carrying short arms. The
striking part is the equality case. Among $11$-vertex trees the extremum is
attained at six of them, exactly where a normalized invariant $\Phi^{11}$ equals
$1$ on the nose, via the integer coincidence
\[
  64 \cdot 243 \cdot 23 \;=\; 621 \cdot 576,
\]
with growth rate $\rho_B = 621/64$. That an optimization over all trees should tie
at an exact integer identity is the first sign of what the campaign eventually
made precise: the obstruction is \emph{arithmetic}, not analytic---there is
provably no smooth certificate, because the continuous relaxation overshoots $1$
between the integers.

\subsection{What is machine-checked}

The analytic core is formalized in Lean~4 against pinned Mathlib, with no
\texttt{sorry} and no added axioms. Reading the library bottom-up, the kernel has
verified:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the permanent-to-matching bridge---$\operatorname{per} L(T)$ equals the
    matching sum for acyclic graphs---and the exact cavity recursion connecting it
    to a \emph{branch model} (\texttt{amplitude\_bridge\_real'}, resting on the
    matching identity and the tree cavity recursion);
  \item the central branch inequality $\Phi \le 1$, \emph{unconditional}
    (\texttt{PotentialFinal.lean:phi\_le\_one}), including the six-point rational
    tie where $\Phi = 1$ exactly;
  \item the arm-extremality ``$g$-step'' at every arity, over achievable
    configurations (\texttt{CappedJointClosure.lean:gstep\_le\_one\_achievable});
  \item a \emph{non-maximality} certificate that sharpens the extremal picture
    (\texttt{R3Cert.Step3.nearStar\_not\_maximal\_at\_five}): at size $56$ a
    broadened configuration---five load-$4$ arms plus five cherries---strictly
    beats the plain near-star of five load-$5$ arms, so the near-star is provably
    \emph{not} the maximizer at that size. The near-star is extremal only in the
    large regime (a broadened tie family interpolates below it, near-star-optimal
    only once $K \ge 23$), which is exactly the sort of integer-tight, size-dependent
    behavior that makes the general problem hard.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is open---and the one subtlety to be careful about}

Two things stand between this and the classical theorem, and we state them
plainly.

First, $\Phi^{11}$ is a \emph{rooted-branch amplitude} invariant. The inequality
$\Phi \le 1$ is proved on that abstract branch model; identifying the branch model
with the classical tree quantity $\operatorname{per} L(T)/\prod \deg(v)$---the
step we call the H2 bridge---is \emph{not} closed at the tree level. (We state
this distinction qualitatively on purpose: the normalization of $\Phi^{11}$ is a
subtle matter---it is set so the invariant equals $1$ at the tie---and it is not
the classical ratio, so we do not attach a single numeral to the comparison.)
Concretely: a reader should not mistake the machine-checked $\Phi \le 1$ for the
classical bound; it is the branch-model statement, and the bridge to the classical
object is among the open pieces.

Second, the global structural assembly (that every tree is dominated by a
near-star of the extremal shape) is not formalized; it remains at the level of
exact finite enumeration and paper argument.

Therefore the classical Brualdi--Goldwasser conjecture is \textbf{open}:
\texttt{conjecture1\_proved = False} throughout the repository, and we make no
claim otherwise. \Cref{tab:bg-status} summarizes.
