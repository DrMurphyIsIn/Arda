\title{Telperion: certificate compilation for Lean~4,\\ and three case studies}
\author{Peter W. Murphy, MD}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Machine-assisted proof increasingly relies on search procedures and language
models that produce plausible but not-always-correct arguments. We describe
\emph{Telperion}, a certificate compiler for Lean~4 built on the opposite
principle: \emph{check the witness, don't trust the author}. You describe a family
of concrete claims; Telperion certifies each instance in exact rational arithmetic
and emits Lean that Mathlib's kernel re-proves from scratch, so a wrong certificate
is a compile error rather than a false theorem. Because the kernel cannot detect a
true-but-vacuous statement, Telperion adds a self-verification layer---per-emitter
sensitivity declarations, forge-the-witness negative controls, and an independent
second checker---and is explicit about the one thing no machinery can settle. We
exercise the system on three deliberately different problems, reporting each at its
true strength: a machine-checked analytic core for the (still open)
Brualdi--Goldwasser tree-maximizer conjecture; four unconditional, kernel-checked
zero-free regions for the Riemann zeta function that are honestly weaker than de la
Vallée-Poussin (we do not claim the Riemann Hypothesis); and a kernel-checked,
symbolic-in-$n$ sum-of-squares lower bound in proof complexity. Throughout,
\texttt{conjecture1\_proved = False} is a tested invariant: we take as much care
with what is not proved as with what is.
\end{abstract}
